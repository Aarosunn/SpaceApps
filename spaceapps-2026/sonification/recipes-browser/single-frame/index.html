One frame, three sounds

One frame of NASA SVS 3912 (sea surface currents coloured by temperature), 34 seconds into the regional movie: eastern North America on the left, the Gulf Stream curling out into the Atlantic. Land is dark grey.

Each pixel's hue was matched to this colour bar to get a temperature. 40% of the pixels have a readable colour (the ocean); the rest are land.

1. Column scan (the naive baseline)

A line sweeps left to right in 6 seconds. Each row of the picture is a pitch (top is high) and its brightness is the loudness. No colour bar is used, only brightness.

Listen for: near-silence while the line crosses dark land, then a wash of sound as it reaches the ocean. It follows the picture but tells you little.

2. North to south: eight notes, then a chord

The picture is cut into eight horizontal strips. Each strip's mean ocean temperature becomes one note, played top to bottom, then all together.

Measured: 7.1, 9.3, 13.6, 19.7, 25.7, 27.6, 28.5, 28.6 °C. Notes: C3, D3, G3, D4, A4, C5, C5, C5.

Listen for: the notes climbing as the water warms towards the south, with the biggest jumps in the middle where the Gulf Stream front is, then levelling off.

3. West to east: a melody across the ocean

The picture is cut into 40 vertical strips. Each strip's mean ocean temperature becomes one note, snapped to a pentatonic scale, and the sound moves from the left ear to the right. A gap is a strip that is all land.

Measured: about 29 °C at the western edge (Gulf of Mexico), falling to about 21 °C at the eastern edge.

Listen for: high notes first, three beats of silence (land), then a descent of nearly two octaves that settles on one low note as the open Atlantic becomes uniform.
