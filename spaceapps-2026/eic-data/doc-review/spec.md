## Summary

Path aliases used in every source field: TEAM = spaceapps-2026 ; EVID = <evidence> ; LIVE = official pages read on 2026-11-14 at about 19:00 UTC in the desktop app's built-in browser (curl cannot validate that site's TLS chain): challenge page https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/the-earth-information-jukebox/ , list page https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/ , judging guide https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/ . (1) The saved challenge text is accurate but incomplete. All 37 non-empty lines of TEAM/sources/jukebox.txt match the live page block for block (SHA-256, 37 of 37). The live page also has three things the file lacks: a 461-character summary whose last sentence says the challenge is to build an Earth Jukebox, 'an interface, script, or application that pairs EIC visual frames with dynamic sonifications generated in real time'; the tags Difficulty Intermediate + Beginner/Youth and Subjects Arts & Multimedia + Earth Science + Software; and a Resources-tab disclaimer. The resource list is unchanged at 11 links. (2) True strength. The challenge text never says must, shall or should. Must-level, because it is phrased as the task itself: an Earth Jukebox that generates dynamic sonifications from NASA EIC visual frames (jukebox.txt L6; live summary). Should-level: the 'desired outcome' that a person can select or experience a specific EIC visualization alongside its corresponding soundscape (L6); real-time generation (live summary only, while the Objectives also allow 'a curated collection of original, generated audio files'); and two 'we encourage' sentences (L4, L7). Everything under Potential Considerations is optional by its own first line (L10 'You may (but are not required to) consider the following'), including every accessibility feature, although the same list states flatly 'Accessibility is a core theme of this challenge' (L17). The real musts are Space Apps rules: 7 slides or a 30-second video, English, references, and submitting before Sunday November 15, 11:59 p.m. local event time. (3) EIC visual frames. Videos count, still images count and downloadable frame folders count (L13, L27, L32, L36). Dashboards are named only as the place where the visuals are used (L27). The text contains no cadence wording at all (no day, daily, date, hour, month, frame rate), so 'one image per day' has no basis in the official text; in the evidence one frame means something different on every page. (4) The 11 resources are 1 gallery (SVS 40467) + 1 deep-space sonification example (13786) + 9 Earth visualization pages. Two links do not match their blurbs: GEOS Aerosols points to 5669, a single still image titled 'GEOS Aerosols (2025)', while the SVS page titled exactly 'GEOS Aerosols' is 5572 (an animation with frame sets and an EIC hyperwall cut); Global Sea Surface Currents and Temperature points at an anchor that is a still image although the blurb says 'These animations'. (5) The team's 02 write-up is exact on titles (11 of 11), URLs (11 of 11), order and count, and 22 of its 27 quoted strings are verbatim. Its faults are strength inflation in four places ('should', 'need', 'Map', 'Expected'), omission of the 'not required' line (L10) and of the 'more resources may be added' line (L25), a truncated data sentence, the phrase 'the 11 listed visuals', the missing subject 'Software', and (inherited from the capture) no knowledge of 'generated in real time'.

## Risks

- Building only pre-rendered audio files. The Objectives allow it (L6), but the summary card that every judge sees says 'dynamic sonifications generated in real time'.
- Driving the sound from data other than the EIC visuals (POWER, GISTEMP, GIBS, or the science dataset behind a visualization) while showing an EIC picture: the Relevance definition penalises NASA data used as 'a superficial addition' and the task sentence says 'from NASA's EIC visual frames'.
- Treating frame number as calendar day. Verified counter-examples: 5669 is one instant; on 5011 time runs unevenly within one movie.
- Naive pixel reading. Frames contain date stamps (5011), labels (5533), a duplicated map (5313 EIC cut), fades to black (5313 at 21 s), rotating or zooming cameras (5011, 5196, 5425) and colours that are aesthetic rather than a data scale (5425 beauty version). The challenge itself warns that 'A direct pixel-to-sound translation might result in chaotic noise' (L20).
- Browser and size limits: SVS media is not readable cross-origin (one URL tested); 5313 exists only as 7680x2160 HEVC or ProRes; frame folders are gigabytes to hundreds of gigabytes and often TIFF or EXR; there is no ffmpeg on the Windows side (per the task brief; the frame extractor runs through WSL).
- Resource traps: 5669 (the official GEOS Aerosols link) is a single still; the anchor for 3912 is a still; Caldwell 73 is deep-space and its audio belongs to SYSTEM Sounds.
- Originality and licensing: audio must be 'original, generated'; 26 crawled pages have movies with their own audio (narration or music) that must not be passed off as the sonification; every library, soundfont, sample and NASA page used has to be listed in References (guide L9).
- Speech is not sonification (L12). A product that mainly reads descriptions aloud misses the task even if it is accessible.
- The 30-second video limit: a soundscape that needs a minute to make sense will not land; slides cannot be trusted to play audio; all linked material must be in English.
- Accessibility claimed but not working (no keyboard path, unlabelled controls, colour-only cues) undermines both the core theme and the Validity score.
- Doing only the mappings the challenge lists (brightness to volume, colour to pitch) puts the project at the baseline for Creativity; the team's own notes record that a 2022 left-to-right scan project stopped at Nominee (TEAM/00-SUMMARY.md L38, not re-verified here).
- Deadline handling: the guide says 'before' 11:59 p.m. local event time; the working machine is on Eastern time while the event is on Central; members cannot be added after submission closes and must not be waitlisted.
- Rules not read in this step: the Project Submission Guide (required fields, AI-use disclosure, link rules). The team's 03 file holds only 2025 or third-party versions of these.
- Using SVS 5572 (the animated GEOS Aerosols) is defensible but it is not the linked page and not in the EIC gallery tree; if used, cite 5669 and 5572 both and say why.

## Ideas

- Satisfy both readings at once: synthesize the sound live in the browser from features of the frame on screen (real time, dynamic), and also export one rendered audio file per visualization with a short process note (the 'curated collection' the Objectives allow).
- Compute per-frame features offline from downscaled, self-hosted copies and ship them as small JSON next to a self-hosted low-resolution video; the browser then only synthesizes. This avoids the cross-origin pixel block, HEVC and multi-gigabyte frames, and scrubbing still changes the sound.
- Limit the jukebox to three to five tracks whose pixel meaning is verified, and follow the blurbs' own hints: 5011 lightning flashes as percussive triggers (L35); 5313 light intensity to volume or tempo with the year label shown as text (L29); 3912 colour as temperature (L30); 5533 four pollutants as four voices (L32); 5669 as a spatial sweep across one still with one timbre per aerosol colour (L31).
- Make the page itself the answer to L6: a list of tracks, each one EIC visualization with its soundscape, a Play control, a plain-language description, and a legend of what each sound means and why.
- Take the accessibility features straight from L17: full keyboard operation, labelled controls and live announcements for screen readers, descriptive text built on NASA's own page descriptions (credited), and vibration on phones as the 'tactile feedback' (an interpretation).
- Mask or crop stamps, labels and colour bars before analysing pixels; read the date stamp separately and expose it as text. Where NASA publishes separate layers (5425 dates-only, land-only and sea-ice layers; 5572 annotation layers) use them instead of guessing.
- Use the EIC cuts (5313, the 7680x2160 versions of 5533 and 5011) when the pitch says 'EIC frames', and handle the duplicated map in the 32:9 frame by analysing one half.
- Plan the 30-second video first: about 5 s on the gap (visual-only Earth science), about 20 s of one track playing with on-screen captions naming the mapping, about 5 s on who benefits. Captions in English.
- Keep a running References list from now: every SVS page and file, every library, soundfont and sample, and the AI tools used.
- Ask the Local Lead or Space Apps Connect one question today: is pre-rendered audio acceptable given 'generated in real time'? Default to live synthesis if no answer comes.
- If the animated aerosols are wanted, use SVS 5572 and state in the write-up that the challenge links 5669 (a still of 2025-08-19) and that 5572 is its related, same-titled animation.
