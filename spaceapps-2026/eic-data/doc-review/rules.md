(2) The 2026 form has 8 fields plus 2 checkboxes; the AI field is now named Use of Super Intelligence (SI); the 9-item list in 03 is the 2025 version. (5) Copyright: no copyrighted material (music is named) without permission; cite everything; third-party materials must be unrestricted or OSI-licensed with licences documented; own work is open-sourced (Apache 2.0 by default). ## Risks

- BITE 1 - MUSIC COPYRIGHT (bites hardest): the originality checkbox names music first among copyrighted materials that need permission (guide-judging.txt L9); the Participant Terms require third-party materials to be unrestricted or OSI-licensed with licences documented, and open-source the team's own output (Apache 2.0 default); the video warranty covers audio. Consequences for a jukebox (my inference): no existing songs, melodies, loops or commercial sample packs; sampled instruments and sound fonts need a licence that allows free redistribution and must be cited; sound synthesised by the team's own code, or CC0/public-domain samples, is the clean path; ML music generators count as SI audio (acknowledge in text and metadata) and their output rights are unclear.
- BITE 1b - NASA SOUNDTRACKS ARE NOT FREE MUSIC: 155 of 559 probed NASA movie files carry audio; six EIC video pages credit commercial production music (Universal Production Music, BBC Production Music) and the SVS 14606 page says its music may not be extracted or remixed; the Caldwell 73 sonification on the challenge-listed page 13786 is credited to SYSTEM Sounds, not NASA. Reusing, sampling or remixing audio from NASA movies is a copyright risk: analyse the frames, generate your own sound.
- BITE 1c - EXTRACTING FRAMES FROM PRODUCED EIC VIDEOS: 8 of 94 SVS pages (14367, 14369, 14507, 14568, 14606, 14696, 14700, 14713) allow sharing the whole video but forbid extracting or remixing their third-party stock imagery. Frame extraction plus sonification is exactly that; if those pages are used the team cannot truthfully tick the permission checkbox.
- BITE 2 - AUDIO IN A 30-SECOND DEMO: the cap is 30 seconds and the official suggested outline spends the first 10 seconds on names, challenge and title, leaving roughly 10-20 seconds in which narration and the sonification compete for one audio channel (inference). The demo must be hosted externally (a host may mute or flag music-like audio - inference), and the project page accepts only images, so judges can hear the result only through the demo link and the final-project link. Going over 30 seconds disqualifies. A 7-slide deck has no defined way to carry audio.
- BITE 3 - SUBTITLES: mandatory only if the video is spoken in a language other than English (guide-judging.txt L6); 00-SUMMARY.md L15 over-states this. But Presentation is judged on the demo and full project: explanations given only as on-screen text exclude the blind audience the challenge names, and an uncaptioned sonification excludes deaf viewers or judges watching muted (inference). Any on-screen text or captions must be in English, and SI-generated captions or descriptions are SI use to disclose.
- BITE 4 - LOGOS INSIDE NASA FRAMES: Space Apps forbids generated content that uses, contains or modifies NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers, and NASA forbids its insignia appearing with SI-generated imagery or in SI training. The evidence shows the meatball in the SVS 4685 end credits (t=309.88 s), worm-logo footage in the EIC b-roll SVS 14371, and a burned-in NASA/GPM label; three sampled challenge-listed frames showed no logo (two carried burned-in labels or a legend). The broader no-NASA-logo rule is unverified (snippet only). Burned-in labels, legends and logos would also be sonified as if they were data unless masked (technical inference).

NASA's guideline: acknowledge NASA, do not imply endorsement, never attribute SI output to NASA. Audio libraries, sound fonts, samples and code libraries must be cited with their licences too.

The challenge calls accessibility a core theme (jukebox.txt L17), Validity scores usability and user-friendliness, and Presentation is judged on the demo and full project; an accessibility-themed entry whose own site is not screen-reader usable, whose demo relies on unread on-screen text, or whose audio sits behind a login or a blocked host undercuts Relevance, Validity and Presentation (inference).
