## Summary

Deep-read of 01-winners-analysis.md (221 lines; notes/A-winners.md is byte-identical, same SHA-256), sources/winners2025.txt (1 line) and sources/hm2025.txt (4 lines), cross-checked against 00-SUMMARY.md, the local 2026 Judging Guide and Jukebox captures, and official NASA / Space Apps pages re-read today (2026-11-14) in the local browser. (1) The two source captures contain boilerplate only and no names, so they prove nothing about winners or honorable mentions; the real lists were read from the live official pages. (2) The 2023, 2024 and 2025 winner tables in 01 are correct (awards, teams, challenges; 2025 team sizes). (3) What separates winners from nominees is documented only structurally: about 1 in 10 submissions becomes a Global Nominee, 45 of 1,294 nominees (about 1 in 29) became finalists in 2025, and about 1 in 4.5 finalists won. Chicago advanced exactly 3 nominees in 2025 and none became a finalist. Project-level differences are team inference from NASA summaries; no comparison set of non-winning nominees exists in these documents. (4) The audio track record is larger than 01 says. Winners confirmed in official sources: SongSAT (2018, Most Inspirational) and Arcobaleno (2023, Global Connection). Additional official finalists: Deep Space Musical (2018, Most Inspirational), Aria EarthMOOG (2018, Best Use of Data), Ten Hours Of Space Music For Sleeping (2023), Saraquatys in G Major (2024). Honorable mentions: Renaissance Quintet (2023), Interstellar Orchestra (2024). SPACETUNE (2022) is a Global Nominee. The Cosmic Keys finalist claim is in no official list. (5) Key correction for Jukebox: an accessibility framing is table stakes, not the twist. It appears in a losing 2018 finalist, in the 2023 brief, and as 'a core theme' of the 2026 brief. The only winner-versus-finalist contrast visible in official write-ups (SongSAT versus Deep Space Musical) is designed, recognizable music versus raw pixel-to-frequency. (6) 01 quotes the 2022 rubric; the 2026 rubric moves completeness and usability into Validity and puts NASA data integration into Relevance. (7) 00-SUMMARY overstates several items: the 'only credible path' with a blind or low-vision listening test, 'most crowded challenge', 'stalled at Cincinnati', and it drops Most Inspirational from the target awards. The 'one image per day' assumption does not appear in any of these documents.

## Risks

- Treating the accessibility framing as the edge: it is in the 2026 brief, the 2023 brief and a losing 2018 finalist, so it will not distinguish the entry.
- Overclaiming user validation: 00 calls a blind or low-vision listening test part of the only credible path, but recruiting such listeners before Sunday night may fail, and presenting sighted listeners as accessibility validation would hurt credibility.
- The 30-second video limit suits audio badly; if judges watch with sound off or skim, the core of the project is invisible unless captions and a legend carry it.
- The local gate is narrow: Chicago advanced 3 of 56 listed teams in 2025 and none went further; a project can be strong and still not be nominated.
- The default build is well trodden: brightness-to-pitch scans and color-to-scale web maps were finalist-grade in 2018 and are the brief's own example, so a plain version will look like many other entries.
- Sound licensing: soundfonts, samples or backing music that are not original or clearly licensed breach the originality declaration (sources/guide-judging.txt:9).
- Rubric drift: 01 and parts of 00 reason from the 2022 criteria wording; the 2026 rubric scores completeness and usability under Validity and NASA data integration under Relevance.
- Thin evidence base: most 'what wins' statements are inferences from NASA press summaries and a few news stories, with no data on losing finalists; the team may over-trust them.
- Drifting off-brief: sonifying time series or non-EIC imagery (as 02 reports many competitor repos do) weakens Relevance, since the objective names EIC visual frames.
- Stale statements in 00 can mislead teammates and other agents: 'most crowded challenge', 'waiting on the official Jukebox text', 'stalled at Cincinnati', and the two-award target.

## Ideas

- LESSON 1 (verified numbers): the first gate is the Chicago room. In 2025 Chicago listed 56 teams and advanced 3 nominees; none reached finalist or honorable mention. Aim to be clearly top 3 locally, and measure the work against the global bar.
- LESSON 2 (verified numbers plus inference): after nomination about 1 in 29 nominees became a finalist in 2025. Global judging works from what is submitted: the project page, a 30-second video or 7 slides, and the linked project (sources/guide-judging.txt:5). Treat those artifacts as the product.
- LESSON 3 (verified text): accessibility is table stakes. Show it in the product with the brief's own examples: screen-reader compatibility, rich descriptive text, tactile feedback (sources/jukebox.txt:17), plus keyboard-only operation. Saying 'for blind users' is what every entry will do.
- LESSON 4 (verified text plus inference from one 2018 pair): make it sound like music with recognizable structure. The brief warns raw pixel-to-sound 'might result in chaotic noise' and suggests scales and harmonies (sources/jukebox.txt:20). SongSAT won with distinct themes per land class; Deep Space Musical, with direct value-to-frequency, stayed a finalist.
- LESSON 5 (verified text): publish the mapping as a legend with the actual rules. The 2023 brief asked teams to explain how the data is sonified, the 2026 brief asks for the 'why' behind choices (sources/jukebox.txt:23), and SongSAT's page did exactly this.
- LESSON 6 (verified text): use EIC frames and name them. The objective requires sonifications 'from NASA's EIC visual frames' (sources/jukebox.txt:6) and the 2026 Relevance descriptor rewards 'NASA data is integral' (sources/guide-judging.txt:27). Show the SVS page id, title and what the frame depicts on screen and in References.
- LESSON 7 (verified rubric): a working, usable build now scores under Validity ('fully developed', 'highly usable and user-friendly', sources/guide-judging.txt:26). Scope to one end-to-end select-and-play flow over a few visualizations.
- LESSON 8 (verified precedent): awards with audio precedent are Most Inspirational (2018 winner and finalist), Global Connection (2023 winner) and Best Use of Data (2018 finalist; its definition includes making space data accessible, sources/guide-judging.txt:12). Art & Technology fits by definition (sources/guide-judging.txt:19) but has no audio precedent in the lists checked. Write the pitch against the chosen definition.
- LESSON 9 (verified): a popular challenge is not disqualifying. 2025 finalists clustered in the most popular challenges (8, 6, 5) and one challenge took 3 of 10 awards; the 2023 sonification challenge placed 2 finalists and 1 honorable mention. Usually only one team per challenge wins, so differentiation inside the challenge is what matters.
- LESSON 10 (verified rule): sound licensing. No copyrighted music, and every external resource goes in References (sources/guide-judging.txt:9). Prefer synthesized sound (Aria used Tone.js) or clearly licensed samples and list them; SongSAT's third-party soundfont would need listing today.
- LESSON 11 (inference): the 30-second video is the hardest constraint for an audio project. Build the demo around a contrast a listener can hear in seconds (two frames or two regions), with captions and an on-screen legend so it still reads with sound off.
- LESSON 12 (inference, honesty): no past audio entry is documented as running a listener test; it is 01's idea for earning Validity. If the team runs one, report exactly who listened and what they were asked, and do not present sighted listeners as blind or low-vision validation.
- LESSON 13 (verified precedent): the default build already exists. Aria (2018) was an interactive web map turning NASA imagery colors into scale notes with Tone.js; it reached finalist for Best Use of Data and did not win. Expect many 2026 teams to rebuild it; go beyond it with per-layer meaning, a legend, real accessibility and EIC frames.
- LESSON 14 (verified text): the brightness and position to volume and pitch mapping is the brief's own model (sources/jukebox.txt:28). Using it is on-brief; it cannot be the differentiator.
