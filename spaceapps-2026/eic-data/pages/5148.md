## Gotchas

- One frame = 1 HOUR, not 1 day; the product is re-rendered once a day.
- Frames 359-371 are valid after the file's write time (07:00:24 UTC 14 Nov), so at least 13 frames are forecast; probably more (unknown).
- Only 2 keyframes in the mp4 (frame 0 and frame 250). Random seeking replays up to 249 frames of 2048x1024 video, so scrubbing is slow: decode once front to back and cache the numbers, or use PNG frames.
- The mp4 index (moov) is at the END of the file: self-host on a server with HTTP Range support.
- Label is US Eastern and the window crosses the 1 Nov 2026 DST change (EDT -> EST); compute in UTC.
- CORS header is fixed to 'https://tempo.multiverse.music' (analyst verified on this page's 00000372.png; verifier on sibling mp4s 5147 and 5150): canvas pixel reads from another origin are blocked. Self-host the files.
- Bundle listing mtime 'Nov 22, 2025' for the frame set disagrees with HTTP Last-Modified 'Fri, 13 Nov 2026 07:30:47 GMT' (00000001.png). The HTTP header wins: the frame set was refreshed on 13 Nov but not yet on 14 Nov.
- Calm wind (below about 4 m/s) has no color: the pixel shows basemap (grey land or dark ocean), so 'no color' means calm, not missing data. Near-black also occurs around 35 m/s inside the strongest storms.
- print.jpg is a static 2023 still (analyst read '15 Aug 2023 09:00:00 EDT'; file Last-Modified 30 Aug 2023).
- searchweb.png is listed as 180x320 (portrait) in the bundle, unlike the sibling pages (320x180).
- Page says frame set has 373 items; listing has 372 files.
- H.264 compression shifts colors slightly; use nearest-color lookup.

Description: Weather models begin their forecasts by integrating observations from ground stations, aircraft, balloons, and a growing constellation of Earth observing satellites to estimate the most realistic atmospheric state. The Goddard Earth Observing System Forward Processing (GEOS-FP) system is a research model maintained by NASA’s Global Modeling and Assimilation Office to demonstrate innovative new ways to use satellite data to improve predictions of weather, air quality, and greenhouse gases and to help NASA collect new observations of our home planet. 
Near surface wind speed is calculated by sampling model fields at 10 meters (about 30 feet) above Earth’s surface. Wind speed is typically largest over ocean because surfaces are smooth and result in less friction than over land. Organized patterns reflect weather fronts and storm systems around the world. Near surface winds help scientists track the movement and severity of storms and air pollution episodes. Increasingly, these kinds of products help wind energy managers plan resources to help meet the world’s demand for energy while reducing the greenhouse gas emissions that cause climate change.
