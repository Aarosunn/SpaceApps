## Quick answer

- What it is: Four 6.42 s movies of 77 plot frames (1950-2026), one frame per hurricane SEASON (year), each frame a time-vs-wind-speed chart of every Atlantic named storm that year.
- Kind: chart-or-graph-animation
- Data source: NOAA HURDAT2 + ATCF best track (credited on page); visualizer Greg Shirah NASA/GSFC (?)
- Period covered: Seasons 1950 through 2026 (current year, in progress). Each frame's x-axis is Jan-Jan (jan_to_dec) or May-Jan (may_to_dec) of one year.
- Time step of the underlying data: Inside each frame: 6-hourly best-track wind (steps visible in lines); between frames: 1 year
- **One frame equals**: One calendar year (hurricane season). Frame index k = year 1950+k. 6.42 s x 12 fps = 77.04 = 77 frames; 1950..2026 = 77 years; frame set has 77 PNGs named ..._1950_12.png ... ..._2026_12.png. Last frame (t=6.42 s) burned-in year label '2026'; first '1950'.
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: 1950 (frame 0)
- Date label seen on screen: Year only, big grey text under the x-axis (e.g. '2026'), plus 'annual storm count: N' bottom-right (13 in 1950, 10 in 2026). Month ticks Jan..Jan on the x-axis (jan_to_dec) or May..Jan (may_to_dec). Title 'Atlantic Hurricane Season'. No NASA logo seen. Y-axis 'wind speed (MPH)' 0-200. Band labels: Tropical Storm, Category-1 ... Category-5. Storm names in cyan (1950 uses Able, Baker, Charlie...; 2026 uses Arthur, Bertha, Cristobal, Dolly, Edouard, Fay, Gonzalo, Hanna, Isaias, Josephine).
- Update behaviour: Page says the current-year plot updates every 2 h in season (bundle line 52). All files carry Last-Modified Nov 14 2026 11:01 GMT (HEAD of 2026 PNG re-checked: 11:01:40, 296,376 bytes), the same bulk-touch minute as 5544, so it is not a render time. The 2026 plot's last storm (Josephine) ends abruptly at ~63 mph about Oct 22 (x=995 of ticks Jan 90 / Jan 1211: (995-90)/(1211-90)*365=295 -> Oct 22), 23 days before today, which hints the 2026 frame is cut off. Frame-folder listing says Oct 16, older than that data: mtimes unreliable.
- Status on 2026-11-14: claims-daily-but-stale — newest data: about 2026-10-22 (last point of storm Josephine, estimated from axis geometry; storm list may be complete up to Nov 14 if no later storm formed)
- View / camera: Flat 2D chart on black, 16:9, no map, no camera motion. Fixed axes frame to frame (identical layout), so pixel positions are stable.
- On-screen clutter: Title, axis labels, tick labels, grey category bands with text, cyan storm-name labels (rotated 45 deg, overlap in busy years), 'annual storm count' text, year label. multiYear variants add ghosted grey traces of strong storms from earlier years (very busy background). No clean/alpha variant exists.
- Fit as a sonification source: **fair** — Not a map and not daily: it is a hand-drawn line chart. Information is better taken from the chart curve (white line height = wind mph, x = day of year) than from a cursor on a picture; works for 'listen to a season' but not for 'listen to a place'. Fixed layout makes the curve easy to extract (white line on dark) but cyan labels and ghost lines add noise.
- Suggested mapping: For a chosen year frame, sweep x (Jan->Dec) as time: pitch = max white-pixel height in that column (wind speed 0-200 mph), and play a drum accent when the line crosses a category band (39 / 74 / 96 / 111 / 130 / 157 mph, approx. positions from the band edges).

## Frame → time mapping

year = 1950 + frame_index (frame_index 0..76) at 12 fps (t = index/12 s). The year is also burned into the bottom of the picture (grey '1950' / '2026').

## Gotchas

- One frame = one YEAR, not one day; there is no daily cadence at all.
- Page says 78 frame items but listing and probe show 77 (1950-2026 inclusive = 77). Trust 77.
- Last-Modified (Nov 14 11:01 GMT) does not mean data of Nov 14: the same 11:01 minute is on 5544's movie. The latest storm data in the 2026 frame is about Oct 22 2026 (Josephine), not Nov 14.
- Frame-folder listing mtime (Oct 16 2026) is older than data it contains (Josephine ends about Oct 22), so file mtimes cannot be used to date content.
- The '_12' in frame names is constant and is not a month or fps.
- The 2026 frame is partial-year and changes; 1950-2025 frames are history.
- jan_to_dec and may_to_dec have different x-axis start; singleYear and multiYear differ in ghost traces. Storm names in the early years (Able, Baker ...) differ from modern lists.
- Print stills (print.jpg) show one frame NASA chose, not necessarily the newest year.
- Old folder frames/5072/g311995 is a 0.00 s only sample; use fresh_* folders.
- Added by the second agent: Group 311995 is described as May-Dec single year but its movie atlantic_jan_to_dec_singleYear.mp4 shows a Jan-Jan axis; May-Dec is only the frame set.
- Added by the second agent: 2026 line for Josephine ends abruptly at ~63 mph about Oct 22 (not decaying like other storms), so the 2026 frame may be cut off 3 weeks before today; all Last-Modified are a bulk 11:01 GMT touch.

Description: These simple visualizations are plots of time vs. wind speed for each tropical storm/hurricane of Atlantic Hurricane seasons from 1950 to the present. Horizontal lines indicate wind speed category thresholds. A line plot for each storm shows the storm's name and a marker at the peak wind speed.
Most named storms during the Atlantic hurricane season happen between June and November.  However, occasionally, storms develop outside of those ranges.
Four versions of the plots are inluded:
   1. May to December showig only the current year
   2. May to December showing the current year and strong storms from previous years (ghosted out)
   3. January to December showig only the current year
   4. January to December showing the current year and strong storms from previous years (ghosted out)
The plot for the current year automatically updates every 2 hours during hurricane season.
