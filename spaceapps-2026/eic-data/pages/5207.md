## Quick answer

- What it is: 30 s, 30 fps colour map animation (Robinson projection) of GISTEMP surface temperature anomalies, 24-month running means, labelled 1881 to 2023, 6 frames per year plus a 48-frame hold on 2023; superseded by 5450 (to 2024).
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: NASA GISS GISTEMP v4 (credited on page) (?)
- Period covered: Labels run 1881 to 2023 (143 labels in time.txt); first map labelled 1881 (24-month running window; first window presumably 1880-1881, inferred); last map 2023 ('final frame represents global temperature anomalies in 2023').
- Time step of the underlying data: Monthly GISTEMP averaged over a running 24-month window, sampled every 2 months (12 months / 6 frames per year, inferred from time.txt).
- **One frame equals**: 2 months of window end date for frames 0 to 851 (6 frames per year), then 48 frames (1.6 s) hold on 2023. Computed from time.txt: 900 lines, 143 distinct labels, 142 labels (1881-2022) with 6 lines each, 2023 with 48 lines (142 x 6 = 852; 852 + 48 = 900).

## Gotchas

- Label is the end year of a 24-month running mean, not a calendar year.
- Frame 0 is labelled 1881, not 1880; each label lasts 6 frames and 2023 is held 48 frames.
- Page says 901 frames for the 4K set; listing has 900 (4K000 to 4K899, 0-based).
- 4K PNG frame set is 4.0 GB, larger than the movie; do not use.
- HD map is Robinson, not equirectangular; use the SOS variant for lat/lon math (movie 119.8 MB).
- Colours saturate at about +2C, so large 2023 Arctic anomalies are flat dark red.
- Newer versions exist (5450 to 2024, 5603 to 2025); this page is two years behind.

## Open questions

- Whether the 1881 label means the first window ends in 1881 (1880-1881); page text does not say.
- Month-level mapping (2 months per frame) is inferred from the 6-frame cadence.
