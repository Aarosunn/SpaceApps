## Quick answer

- What it is: 30 s, 60 fps square movie: a spiral of monthly global temperature anomalies (GISTEMP v4, baseline 1951-1980) builds from 1880 to Oct 2026 (inferred), then the camera pulls back; movies re-rendered 2026-11-13.
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: NASA GISS GISTEMP v4 monthly global surface temperature anomaly (credited on page) (?)
- Period covered: Jan 1880 to present. Movie fetched today: centre year label reaches 2026 and the spiral tip stops at the OCT position (9 o'clock) in 2026 (inferred newest month: Oct 2026). Spiral builds in the first ~23.4 s; remaining ~6.6 s is a camera pull-back to a side view with year labels 1880...2026.
- Time step of the underlying data: Monthly anomaly (one vertex per month; angle = calendar month, radius = anomaly; ring = one year).
- **One frame equals**: About 1.26 months (inferred: 6.3 years/s x 12 / 60 fps), i.e. ~0.8 frame per month, ~9.5 frames per year. Not a day.
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: 1880
- Date label seen on screen: Big centre year (1880, 1892, 1942, 1999, 2018, 2024, 2026) coloured by that year's anomaly; month names JAN to DEC around the ring; ring labels +1C, 0, -1C; side view has green year labels 1880, 1900 ... 2000, 2026 and axis labels +1C, 0C. English/Celsius main movie; separate English/Fahrenheit and Spanish/Celsius movies. No NASA logo seen.
- Update behaviour: Re-rendered on a schedule: page says updated roughly two weeks after the end of each month. All three movies Last-Modified 2026-11-13 22:52:45 to 22:53:12 GMT (17:52-17:53 US Eastern); page record updated 2026-11-13 17:57 Eastern; print stills 22:55-22:57 GMT. The whole movie is rewritten (30 s fixed length, so years/s drifts). The EXR frame set En_C is stale: modified 2026-09-11 10:02-10:04 AM, 63 days before the mp4. Lag: tip at Oct 2026 means ~13 days after month end (inferred). Window: none, always whole 1880-present record.
- Status on 2026-11-14: updating-irregularly
- View / camera: Polar plot seen from above (months clockwise, JAN at top), 2160x2160, 3D-lit tube spiral; last ~6 s the camera tilts to a side view of stacked rings. Not a map.
- On-screen clutter: Yellow +1C and -1C rings, green 0C ring, month labels, large centre year; tubes overlap heavily after ~1950. No clean variant.
- Clean variant: No. Variants only by language and unit: English degC, English degF, Spanish degC.
- What the colours mean: Line colour = anomaly vs 1951-1980: white near 0, blue below, pink/red above (dark crimson for the warmest). Radius also encodes the anomaly (green ring 0C, yellow rings +1C and -1C).
- Fit as a sonification source: **fair** — Single global series encoded in polar geometry with overlapping tubes and a camera move, so a pixel reader needs polar-to-month conversion and tip tracking; better rebuilt from GISTEMP numbers than read from pixels, though the 12-note yearly cycle is intuitive to the ear.
- Suggested mapping: Follow the leading tip: angle = month (note in a 12-step scale), radius from the green 0C ring = anomaly (pitch offset), centre year as slow drone.

## Frame → time mapping

Approximate formula from five labelled samples: months_since_Jan1880 = 1.26 x frame for frames 0 to ~1400 (60 fps); year = 1880 + months/12. Frames after ~1400 are camera moves with no new data. Year exists only as burned-in centre text (file names carry a frame number only); month = angle of the leading tip. No date-only frame set.

## Gotchas

- Do not use the EXR frame set En_C: dated 2026-09-11, 63 days older than the mp4, and 8.2 GB.
- Movie length is fixed at 30 s, so each monthly update changes years per second and the frame-to-date formula.
- Year label is the only text date; month must be inferred from tip angle.
- Year label colour changes with anomaly, so OCR must be colour-robust.
- After ~23.4 s the picture is a camera pull-back; no new data.
- Final data month is not printed anywhere; Oct 2026 is an inference.

##### Media group 372786 | widget: Basic text with HTML | title: (none)
Description: The visualization presents monthly global temperature anomalies. This visualization is updated roughly two weeks after the end of each month.
Temperature anomalies are deviations from a long term global average. In this case the period 1951-1980 is used to define the baseline for the anomaly. These temperatures are based on the GISS Surface Temperature Analysis (GISTEMP v4), an estimate of global surface temperature change. The data file used to create this visualization is publicly accessible here.
The term 'climate spiral' describes an animated radial plot of global temperatures. Climate scientist Ed Hawkins from the National Centre for Atmospheric Science, University of Reading popularized this style of visualization in 2016.
The Goddard Institute for Space Studies (GISS) in New York is a NASA laboratory managed by the Earth Sciences Division of the agency’s Goddard Space Flight Center in Greenbelt, Maryland. The laboratory is affiliated with Columbia University’s Earth Institute and School of Engineering and Applied Science in New York.
