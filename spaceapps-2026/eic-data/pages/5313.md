## Quick answer

- What it is: A 43.33 s, 7680x2160 (32:9) loop: 12 annual-average Black Marble night-light maps (2012 to 2023) flash by in the first 6.4 s, the lights fade into a purple/orange map of the 2012-to-2023 change, then the same sequence repeats with four zoomed pop-out boxes (Western Europe, Eastern Mediterranean, Ukraine, India). Every frame shows the whole world twice side by side. It is 13 distinct pictures, not one image per day.
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: NASA Black Marble night lights from the VIIRS instrument on the Suomi NPP satellite, shown as annual averages. VERIFIED in bundles/5313.md line 15 ('Black Marble: Next Generation | common name: Black Marble | platform: Suomi NPP | sensor: VIIRS') and line 8 (keywords Black Marble, Night lights, VIIRS). Visualizer Kel Elkins (USRA); five Black Marble scientists are credited (bundle lines 19-20). The page does NOT name the product ID or the processing, and its dataset link points to the 2016 Black Marble colour maps, not to the annual composites that are shown. (observation)
- Period covered: Calendar years 2012 to 2023 as 12 annual averages (VERIFIED: every label 2012, 2013, ... 2023 was read off the decoded frames), plus one derived map of the change between 2012 and 2023. No month, day or time of day exists anywhere in this product.
- Time step of the underlying data: 1 year (annual average); 12 steps. The change map is one still picture (2023 compared with 2012) with no time step.
- **One frame equals**: Nothing fixed - this is not a regular time-lapse. The movie has 1300 frames at 30 fps (1300/30 = 43.33 s) but only 13 distinct data pictures (12 annual maps + 1 change map), each shown twice. While the years step, one data step (1 year) is held for 17 or 18 movie frames: the label changes at frames 15, 33, 51, 69, 86, 104, 122, 139, 157, 175, 193, so the mean hold is (193-15)/10 = 17.8 frames = 0.593 s per year; nominally 1 frame = 1/17.8 year = 20.5 days, but the picture does not move inside a hold (lights and label switch together within 1-2 frames, no cross-fade between years). Exceptions: 2012 is held 15 frames (0.50 s) in pass 1 and 187 frames (6.23 s) in pass 2; 2023 is held 83 frames (2.77 s) at full strength in each pass. Of the 1300 frames, 724 (55.7%) show a year at full strength, 491 (37.8%) show the static change map and 85 (6.5%) are fades.
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: VERIFIED options: (1) compute it from the frame index with the table or formula in frame_to_time_mapping (recommended; exact); (2) OCR the burned-in 4-digit year (present only on k=0-289 and k=630-1104). File names never contain the year: the movie names have none and the EXR names carry only a render frame number (for example ...EIC.02599.exr). There is no date-only or 'dates' frame set on this page (one frame folder only). On the alternate page 5276 the year IS in the file name (nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k_only_lights.2012.tif ... .2023.tif and nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k.02012.tif ... .02023.tif).
- Date label seen on screen: Yes, year only: a light-grey 4-digit label (2012 ... 2023) just right of the centre seam, at about x 3980-4155, y 1070-1145 of the 7680x2160 frame (x 663-692, y 178-191 in the 1280x360 previews). No month, day, clock time or time zone. It is absent during the change map (k=290-629 and 1105-1299), where the only date text is the colour-bar title 'Change in Lighting Intensity from 2012 to 2023'.
- Update behaviour: Static: rendered once and never refreshed. Evidence: EXR header capDate 2024:05:30 15:21:38 (render time); upload Last-Modified values all on 2024-06-14 UTC (EXR frames 19:08:45 to 19:17:54, h265 mp4 19:09:42, ProRes 19:10:35, searchweb and thumbnail 19:51, print.jpg 19:53:06); page released 2024-06-14 and page record last updated 2024-06-14T15:53:06-04:00 (= 19:53:06 UTC). No file is newer, so nothing has changed for 883 days up to 2026-11-14 (365 + 365 + 153). Not a rolling window; the newest data is the 2023 annual average; there is no lag to report.
- Status on 2026-11-14: static — newest data: 2023 (annual average for calendar year 2023; no finer date exists)
- View / camera: Flat equirectangular, fixed geography and camera; the 7680x2160 frame holds the whole world twice as two 3840x2160 tiles (10.67 px per deg lon, 12 px per deg lat). Left edge of each tile is about -168.6 deg (+/-0.4); the analyst's -168.4 +/-0.3 and the team's -168.8 both fall inside that error (about 4 px apart). lon = -168.6 + 360*(x mod 3840)/3840, lat = 90 - 180*y/2160. The offset is a fit, not exact.
- On-screen clutter: No NASA logo, title, credits or numeric scale anywhere. (1) Year label just right of the centre seam, so it lies entirely in the right tile (k=0-289 and 630-1104). (2) Colour bar across the centre seam at about x 3764-4370, y 1180-1285 (k=290-629 and 1105-1299): title 'Change in Lighting Intensity from 2012 to 2023', captions 'lights out', 'no change', 'new lights'; no numbers. (3) From k=670 to the last frame, four pop-out boxes with thin white outlines, a small source rectangle on the map and a translucent connector wedge, titled 'Western Europe' and 'Eastern Mediterranean' (left tile) and 'Ukraine' and 'India' (right tile). Together they alter 22.5% of the frame. The boxes are magnified copies, so their lights are bigger and brighter than on the map (whole-frame mean luma jumps 42.8% when they appear), and they hide Central/South America and most of Asia on the left tile and eastern North America and the Indian Ocean on the right tile.
- Clean variant: Yes by cropping; no as a separate official file on this page. VERIFIED: the left tile (crop 3840x2160 at x=0) of any frame k<=289 is a complete world map with no text at all (checked on frame 7), because the year label lies in the right tile; in the first change-map hold (k=305-615) the left tile only catches the left 76 px of the colour bar at its right edge (x 3764-3839, y 1180-1285). Page 5313 offers no label-free movie, no date-free frame set and no separate layers (the EXR frames do have an alpha channel; its content was not inspected). Purpose-made clean files are on alternate page 5276 (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5276/, checked live today, not in the evidence bundle), all under https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a005200/a005276/ : 12 lights-only TIFFs on black, one per year with the year in the name (16-bit RGBA with alpha); the same 12 frames as a 1.07 MB movie nightlights_flat_series_stills_only_lights_2160p3_h265.mp4 (frame i = year 2012+i, but a small year label is burned in); 12 with-basemap TIFFs nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k.02012.tif ... .02023.tif (62 MB each as listed); 8K JPEG stills, HEAD-checked but not opened: change map without colour bar nightlights_flat_change_still_8k.00700.jpg (2648109 bytes), change map on black nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k_only_lights.change.jpg (1956065 bytes), 2012 and 2023 comparison images nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k_compare.02012.jpg (2466015 bytes) and .02023.jpg (2606413 bytes); the colour bar alone nightlights_change_colorbar.png (3840x2160, 684708 bytes).
- What the colours mean: Time-series part: warm yellow-white points = night-light brightness from the annual average (brighter and whiter = more light); no legend, no units; values are display-scaled, not radiances. They sit on a very dark grey land / black ocean base map (ice sheets lighter grey). Change part: diverging colours on the same base map - orange = less light in 2023 than in 2012 (bar caption 'lights out'), white = 'no change', purple = more light ('new lights'). The bar has no numbers or units.
- Fit as a sonification source: **good** — Good as a SPATIAL source, weak as a time series. Fixed geography, a nearly black background (whole-frame mean luma about 8 of 255) and sparse bright points make brightness easy to turn into sound, the change map has a clear two-sided meaning (purple = more light, orange = less), and the challenge text names this page. But there are only 13 distinct pictures, and year-to-year differences are small: measured on the movie, whole-frame mean luma rises only 2.98% from 2012 to 2023 (7.946 to 8.183), the Asia box (about 54-134 deg E) 9.6%, the share of bright pixels 43% (0.0757% to 0.1083% at 960x270). A naive 'frame brightness to volume' mapping on the movie would mostly play the fades and the zoom-box pop-out (+42.8% luma at frames 670-686), not the data. The page's own files are awkward (8K-wide HEVC, 4.29 GB ProRes, 1300 EXR files of 21-36 MB), so work from 13 extracted stills or from the 5276 per-year files.
- Suggested mapping: Treat it as 12 yearly images + 1 change image, not as video. Take one 3840x2160 tile (left half, frames without boxes). Sweep a playhead west to east across the 360 deg of longitude; for each longitude column sum the light in latitude bands: band = pitch on a pentatonic scale (north = high), summed brightness = loudness and note density, longitude = stereo pan. Play the 12 years as 12 bars so growth (India, China, the Gulf) becomes audibly denser. On the change map use two timbres: purple (more light) = bright bell rising, orange (less light) = soft low tone falling. Speak the year and the region under the playhead as text for screen-reader users.

## Gotchas

- NOT one image per day. The page holds 12 annual-average maps (2012-2023) and 1 change map; each year is on screen for about 0.6 s and the whole sequence is shown twice in 43.33 s. Any design that assumes a daily (or any regular) cadence is wrong for this page.
- The world is drawn twice in every frame (two 3840x2160 tiles). Whole-frame statistics double-count; analyse one tile. The left tile has no year label.
- From frame 670 (22.33 s) to the end, four magnified boxes alter 22.5% of the frame. For global analysis use frames 0-642; the boxes are useful only for regional close-ups.
- Year timing is irregular: holds of 15, 18, 18, 18, 17, 18, 18, 17, 18, 18, 18 frames, then 2023 for 83 frames. Do not assume 0.5 s, 0.6 s or 1 s per year; use the frame table. In pass 2 the same changes occur exactly 815 frames later.
- Year-to-year differences are subtle (whole-frame mean luma +2.98% from 2012 to 2023; some steps are flat, for example Asia box 2019 11.703 and 2020 11.703), while HEVC keyframe ripple alone moves box means by about 0.5% every 30 frames. Brightness is display-scaled with no units and no numeric colour bar, so pixel values are not radiances.
- Awkward files: the only movie under 4 GB is 7680x2160 HEVC (not reliably playable in browsers; not tested here). The ProRes .mov is 4.29 GB and runs at 29.97 fps (43.38 s) while the HEVC runs at 30 fps (43.33 s): same 1300 frames, slightly different timestamps.
- EXR frame set: the folder itself answers HTTP 403, and NASA's page gives two wrong counts for it ('[0 Item(s)]' in the description, '[131072 Item(s)]' in the download list). Files do exist by exact name with ODD numbers only, 00001 to 02599 (even numbers and 02600 give 404), so 1300 files (INFERRED from 17 odd-number hits and 5 misses). Each is 21.3 MB before the boxes appear and 33.8-35.6 MB after, about 36 GB in total (670 x 21.3 MB + 630 x 34 MB). OpenEXR half-float RGBA, ZIPS compression: not readable by browsers.
- frames.sh / ffmpeg -ss timestamps are not always exact on this movie: a requested time less than about 0.1 s before a whole second returns the frame AT the whole second (keyframes every 30 frames, INFERRED). Three files in frames/5313/extra_fine1 are therefore mislabelled: f2_t2.95.jpg shows t=3.0, f3_t5.95.jpg shows t=6.0 and f9_t9.9.jpg shows t=10.0 (byte-identical to the frames taken at those times). For frame-exact work decode sequentially and count frames.
- Between segments the picture is briefly only the dark base map (around k=289-290, 629-630 and 1104-1105), and the loop point (k=1299 back to k=0) is a hard cut from the change map with boxes to 2012 without boxes.
- Each tile is the full 360 x 180 deg squeezed into 16:9 (10.67 px per deg of longitude, 12 px per deg of latitude), and the seam is near 168.4 deg W, not at the date line or at Greenwich. The longitude offset is a fit (+/-0.3 deg), not a published value.
- The page's dataset credit links to the 2016 Black Marble colour maps on visibleearth.nasa.gov; it does not identify the annual 2012-2023 product that is actually shown.
- Folders frames/5313/extra_alt5276_onlylights, extra_alt5276_main and extra_alt5475_sos hold previews of the ALTERNATE pages 5276 and 5475, not of 5313.
- The three alternates differ: 5276 = the original 16:9 version (one world map, only Ukraine and India boxes, plus many stills and per-year frames); 5475 = Science On a Sphere 2:1 version released 2025-05-16, whose 2048x1024 movie is MPEG-4 Part 2 (mp4v, not browser-playable), 42.00 s, with the year printed four times and a slower cadence (2012 at 0.30 s, 2016 at 8.54 s, 2020 at 16.78 s, 2023 at 25.02 s); 5477 = a different animation (day/night cycle over three days in 2024), not examined.
- Added by the second agent: Team doc 06-eic-data-breakdown.md line 12 says 'One frame is ... 1 year (night lights)'. That holds per data picture, not per movie frame: a movie frame is 1/30 s, each year-picture is held 17-18 frames (about 0.6 s), and 2023 is held 83 frames. Use the frame table or extract stills; do not index the movie by year.
- Added by the second agent: The 5276 alternate assets (mp4, only_lights TIFFs, folder) are in no local bundle; only live HEADs support them (mp4 34264467 B, Last-Modified 20 May 2024; h265 1066433 B; 2012 TIFF 8982056 B). That 5276 follows the 5313 timeline rests on 3 frames, one of them (30.0 s) the last frame of the 2016 hold, so a 1-frame shift would be invisible.
- Added by the second agent: The page does not say whether 2012 and 2023 are full calendar years of data; it only says 'annual averages from 2012 to 2023'. Do not label the data Jan-Dec in the UI.
- Added by the second agent: Seam longitude: -168.4 (analyst) and -168.8 (spec) are both inside the measurement error (my 14-city test gives about -168.7 +/-0.4). The offset is fitted, not published; a roughly 4 px difference is below the size of a city blob.
