## Gotchas

- One frame = 1 HOUR, not 1 day. 'Daily' in 'Daily Visualizations' means re-rendered once a day. A true one-value-per-day series must be built by averaging 24 frames (14 complete UTC days are available: frames 15-350).
- Frames 359-371 (valid 08:00-20:00 UTC on 14 Nov) are later than the file's write time (07:00:15 UTC), so they cannot be analysis of observed weather: at least 13 forecast frames. More of the tail is probably forecast too (unknown how many). 'Forecast' is an inference from the timestamps; the page does not say it.
- Only 2 keyframes in the mp4 (frame 0 and frame 250; MP4 stss = [1, 251]). Jumping to an arbitrary frame makes the decoder replay up to 249 frames of 2048x1024 video, so scrubbing or random access in a <video> element is slow. Decode once front to back and cache the per-frame numbers, or use the PNG frames.
- The mp4 index (moov) is at the END of the file (not fast-start). Self-host on a server that supports HTTP Range requests, or the browser must download the whole file before it can play.
- Date label is US Eastern, and the current window crosses the DST change: local label '01 Nov 2026 01:00:00' appears twice (EDT then EST). Do date math in UTC.
- CORS: the server answers 'access-control-allow-origin: https://tempo.multiverse.music' to any Origin (VERIFIED on this page's mp4 with Origin http://localhost:5173). A page on another origin can play the video in a plain <video> tag but canvas pixel reads will be blocked (tainted canvas) and fetch() will fail. Download the mp4/PNGs and serve them from the app's own origin.
- The frame folder's HTML listing says every file was modified 'Nov 22, 2025' (re-fetched today, still says so), but HTTP HEAD returns Last-Modified 13 Nov 2026 and the frames show 29 Oct - 13 Nov 2026 dates. The files win: the frame set is refreshed, and the listing is stale.
- Frame set and mp4 are out of step by 24 h today (frame set = 13 Nov window, mp4 = 14 Nov window). Do not mix them without re-anchoring dates.
- print.jpg / searchweb / thm stills are from 2023: print.jpg shows '15 Aug 2023 09:00:00 EDT'. Do not use them as 'today'.
- NASA text disagrees with itself on height: page description says '3 meters above Earth's surface', the long description says '2 meters (about 6 feet)', the colorbar says 'T2M'.
- Page says the frame set has 373 items; the listing has 372 PNG files.
- mp4 is lossy: colors are slightly shifted by H.264 compression, so nearest-color lookup against the colorbar is needed, not exact match.
- Timestamp seeking with frames.sh returns the first frame at or after t, so a rounded t can land one frame later than expected. For exact frames use the PNG set or seek to (i + 0.5)/12 s.

Description: Weather models begin their forecasts by integrating observations from ground stations, aircraft, balloons, and a growing constellation of Earth observing satellites to estimate the most realistic atmospheric state. The Goddard Earth Observing System Forward Processing (GEOS-FP) system is a research model maintained by NASA’s Global Modeling and Assimilation Office to demonstrate innovative new ways to use satellite data to improve predictions of weather, air quality, and greenhouse gases and to help NASA collect new observations of our home planet. Near surface temperature is calculated by sampling model fields at 2 meters (about 6 feet) above Earth’s surface, helping scientists communicate to the general public the air temperatures they encounter. Variations reflect differences in daytime and nighttime temperatures and the influence of large scale weather systems. As the planet warms in response to rising greenhouse gas concentrations, scientists are also tracking changes in the frequency of heat waves and their potential impacts on human health.
