## Quick answer

- What it is: Official challenge resource. ECCO2 ocean-model animation: surface-current flow lines coloured by sea-surface temperature on a flat world map, 2007-03-25T12:00Z to 2008-03-03T12:00Z (344 days) in 2001 frames; one frame = 4.128 hours, NOT one day; rendered once in 2012 and never updated.
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: ECCO2 (Estimating the Circulation and Climate of the Ocean, Phase II; joint MIT/JPL project): the MITgcm ocean and sea-ice model constrained by satellite and in-situ data. Only surface currents (flow lines) and sea-surface temperature (colour) are shown; land is GTOPO30 shaded relief. It is model output (a state estimate), not a direct satellite observation. Source: bundles/3912.md lines 12, 15-16. Credit line requested on the page: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio. (model)
- Period covered: VERIFIED: model time 2007-03-25T12:00Z to 2008-03-03T12:00Z = exactly 344 days (about 11.3 months; 22 days short of a full year; includes 29 Feb 2008). Four NASA sources agree: the page text for media group 349713, the per-frame table flat_ecco2_dates.txt (first and last line), the dates burned into the Pacific variant (first frame 3/25/2007, last frame 3/03/2008) and the hyperwall caption image. Exception: the Atlantic TIFF frame set stops at file 29996 = 2008-01-20T07:52Z (1750 frames, about 300.8 days).
- Time step of the underlying data: Frames are 14,860.8 s = 4.128 h apart in model time (VERIFIED from NASA's per-frame table). That is the rendering step. The cadence of the underlying ECCO2 output is not stated on the page or in the API record: unknown. INFERENCE only: daily-mean fields interpolated in time, because the first and last stamps are both 12:00Z and the span is exactly 344.000 days (ECCO2 is commonly distributed as daily or 3-day averages; that is general knowledge, not evidence from this page).
- **One frame equals**: 4.128 hours of model time (4 h 07 min 40.8 s = 14,860.8 s = 0.172 day). Arithmetic: 344 days x 24 = 8256 h; 2001 frames = 2000 intervals; 8256 / 2000 = 4.128 h. Cross-check from burned-in dates: 3/25/2007 at frame 0 and 3/03/2008 at frame 2000 = 344 days / 2000 = 0.172 day. So 5.814 frames per day; 1 s of 30 fps video = 30 x 4.128 h = 123.84 h = 5.16 days; one day = 0.194 s; one week = 1.36 s; the whole 66.70 s = 344 days.
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: Best: NASA's lookup table https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a003900/a003912/flat_ecco2_dates.txt (plain text, 50,025 bytes, 2001 lines of '<file number>  <YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MMZ>'), linked from media group 349717. Equivalent: the formula in frame_to_time_mapping (exact, no file needed). Third: the burned-in date label, present only in the Pacific overlay variant (OCR possible but unnecessary). NOT from file names: they hold render counters, not dates. There is no separate date-only frame set.

Colour-bar version (comp movie, with_colorbar TIFF): colour bar only, no date. - View / camera: Main product: flat equirectangular (plate carree) world map, aspect 2:1, longitude -180 to +180 with Greenwich in the centre, latitude +90 at the top to -90 at the bottom; geography fixed, no camera motion (continents sit at identical pixels in frames at t = 0, 16.0, 32.8, 49.6, 65.6 s). - Clean variant: Yes. The default flat global movies and the orig / 6840x3420 TIFF frames carry nothing but the map. For the Pacific view the clean pair is frames/1920x1080_16x9_30p/pacific_bare/ (PNG) and pacificcurrents_no_overlay_1080p30_2.mp4. There are no alpha or separate layers: PNG frames are 8-bit RGB, TIFF frames are 8-bit RGB (orig is uncompressed), and land, black background and flow lines are baked into one image. The colour bar is available on its own as sst_ecco2_cbar.png (310x95, RGBA).
- What the colours mean: Hue of a flow line = modelled sea-surface temperature on a rainbow scale from 0 deg C (blue) to 32 deg C (red); NASA's caption gives blue 0, green 10-20, yellow about 25, red 32. RGB sampled from NASA's sst_ecco2_cbar.png, assuming even spacing between the 0/10/20/32 ticks (the tick spacing supports this): 0=(52,92,250), 4=(50,154,204), 8=(53,209,158), 10=(56,232,135), 12=(59,252,113), 16=(79,255,71), 20=(179,255,38), 24=(251,248,33), 28=(248,138,27), 32=(247,0,24). Hue falls monotonically from 228 degrees (0 deg C) to -6 degrees (32 deg C), so hue alone inverts to temperature; it changes slowest between 10 and 16 deg C. The line itself = path of the surface current (flow direction and eddies). Brightness is trail rendering; the page does not say it encodes speed. Grey = land relief, black = no flow line.
- Fit as a sonification source: **excellent** — Easiest picture in the set to decode honestly: fixed flat map, no text to mask, a documented colour scale (hue -> 0-32 deg C), an exact frame -> date formula backed by NASA's own table, and a 15.7 MB browser-native movie. A spot test on extracted frames turned hue into 30-degree band temperatures that follow the seasons (30-60N: 12.9 deg C on 2007-03-25, 19.9 on 2007-09-10, 12.8 on 2008-02-26; 30-60S: 13.1, 9.4, 13.3), which gives a clear, explainable musical arc. Limits: change is slow and continuous (no discrete events for percussion), current speed is not directly readable from pixels, and it is one modelled year (2007-08), not live data.
- Suggested mapping: Cut the flat map into 6 latitude bands of 30 degrees. For each analysed frame keep pixels with saturation > 0.35 and max(R,G,B) > 40 (this drops grey land and black gaps), convert hue to deg C with the colour-bar table, and average per band (brightness-weighted). One sustained voice per band: temperature -> pitch on a pentatonic scale (0 deg C lowest, 32 deg C highest), band -> register or stereo position (north to south), share of lit flow pixels in the band -> loudness. Announce the date from the formula about once per data month (every 5.9 s at normal speed) and add a keyboard probe that speaks region, deg C and date for screen-reader users.

## Frame → time mapping

Let k = 0-based frame index, 0..2000. time(k) = 2007-03-25T12:00:00Z + k x 14860.8 s (use real UTC date arithmetic; 2008 is a leap year). Flat TIFF file number n = 23000 + 4k, i.e. time(n) = 2007-03-25T12:00:00Z + (n - 23000) x 3715.2 s. Pacific PNG number = k (pacificcurrents.00000 to .02000). museum/Oceanflows.NNNN.tif number = k + 1. Movies are 30 fps: frame k is on screen from k/30 to (k+1)/30 s, so k = floor(t x 30); in a browser, requestVideoFrameCallback gives mediaTime and k = Math.round(mediaTime x 30). Movies with 2061 or 2031 frames hold the last picture at the end: k = min(k, 2000). Checks: the formula reproduces all 2001 lines of NASA's table (0 mismatches; the table truncates to the minute); n = 25000 (k = 500, t = 16.67 s) -> 2007-06-19T12:00Z; k = 1000 (t = 33.33 s) -> 2007-09-13T12:00Z; k = 2000 (t = 66.67 s) -> 2008-03-03T12:00Z. Exact for the Pacific movie (burned-in dates match at k = 0, 2, 3, 1000, 2000); for the flat movie the alignment is confirmed only to within a few frames (NASA's still of file 25000 best matches movie frames around k = 500).

## Gotchas

- NOT one frame per day: 4.128 h per frame, 5.814 frames per day. Each UTC date is shared by 5 or 6 consecutive frames (3 on the first and last date). To step one day, advance 5.814 frames (0.194 s of video).
- Covers 344 days, not a year: a looped movie jumps from 2008-03-03 back to 2007-03-25 (22 days of the seasonal cycle are missing).
- Model output for 2007-08, not an observation and not current. Any user-facing description should say 'ECCO2 ocean model, March 2007 to March 2008'.
- File-name numbers are render counters, not dates: flat TIFF 23000..31000 step 4; Pacific PNG 00000..02000 step 1; museum/Oceanflows.0001..2001 (1-based). NASA's names say 2028x1024 but the images are 2048x1024; URLs must use the names as spelled.
- Three movie lengths. 2001 frames / 66.70 s: ecco2_sst_flow (.webmhd.webm, .m4v, .mp4), Oceanflows-UHD-2160.mp4, pacificcurrents_1080p30 (.webm, .mp4). 2061 frames / 68.70 s: flat_global_ecco2_2028x1024_comp_p30.mp4 and all 16 hyperwall tile movies. 2031 frames / 67.70 s: pacificcurrents_no_overlay_1080p30_2.mp4. The extra 60 or 30 frames are the last picture held still at the end (checked on the comp MP4, the tile b1 WebM and the no_overlay MP4), not more data; that the hold starts exactly after frame 2000 is inferred from the counts.
- CORS blocks pixel access from a local page: every file is served with 'access-control-allow-origin: https://tempo.multiverse.music' (unchanged when the request sends Origin: http://localhost:5173). A page can display the video or image straight from NASA, but canvas reads throw a security error and fetch() of the date table fails. Download the files once and serve them from the app's own origin over http://localhost (a file:// page also taints the canvas in Chrome).
- The challenge link https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3912/#media_group_349716 lands on a single still (flow lines plus white dots marking model grid points: ecco2_and_grid.jpg, 6840x3420, 13,471,763 bytes; also .tif 36.1 MB and _web.png 320x160), although the challenge text calls the resource 'These animations'. The animations are media groups 349713 (flat global), 349717 (hyperwall tiles) and 349721 (Pacific). The still's date is not stated; pixel comparison puts it at or near the last animation frame (about 2008-03-03), unverified.
- Page text versus files (files win): the page lists ecco2_sst_flow.webmhd.webm as 960x540 but the stream is 1080x540; page item counts for frame sets are one too high (2002 vs 2001 files; 1751 vs 1750; 2004 vs 2001 TIFF plus 2 extra entries); the dataset credit attaches '3-2007 through 2-2008' to GTOPO30 topography and no range to ECCO2, while the frames run to 2008-03-03; media group 349717 says cut-up frames are downloadable but only movies and stills are listed; the page carries an unrelated GPM mission tag; size labels on the page are binary megabytes (the 14.9 MB label = 15,653,144 bytes).
- Pixel reading: flow lines are 1-2 px wide, vary in brightness and sit on black; land is grey, not black. Classify by saturation and brightness first, then use hue only. Video compression (4:2:0, 1.9 Mb/s in the small WebM) smears single lines, so average over blocks or bands. In the spot test, band means from the small WebM, the 256 MB MP4 and NASA's JPEG still agreed to within about 1.3 deg C.
- Motion per frame is tiny (inferred: comparison scores against NASA's still were flat across frames 499-502 at 2048 px wide), so frame-to-frame differences are a weak signal. Analyse every 6th frame (about one day) or use regional statistics.
- Avoid the colour-bar and UHD versions for analysis: in the spot test the colour bar made the 60-90S band read 12.1 deg C instead of about 2, and the UHD movie's black bars shift every latitude band. Oceanflows-UHD-2160.mp4 also carries an AAC stereo track that is digital silence (-91 dB in a 4 s sample at t = 30 s).
- Frame sets are huge and mostly not browser-decodable: orig TIFF 2001 x 6,300,088 bytes = 12.6 GB; with_colorbar TIFF about 3.7 MB per file; 6840x3420 TIFF about 33-35 MB per file (roughly 66-70 GB); Atlantic TIFF about 22 MB per file, only 1750 frames (23000..29996) and no movie; Pacific PNG about 2.66 MB per file (about 5.3 GB per set). Use a movie, or fetch every Nth frame only.
- frames/6840x3420_1x1_30p/ also holds an unlisted museum/ subfolder (2001 TIFF, Oceanflows.0001-2001, 1-based numbering) and a 'todo' entry that returns HTTP 404. NASA's date table truncates to the minute (file 23004 is listed as 16:07Z, exact value 16:07:40.8Z).
- Added by the second agent: Spec line 19 labels f23000.tif 'March 2007 frame'. NASA's table says f23000 = 2007-03-25T12:00Z, the very first frame of a 344-day run. f23024 = 2007-03-26T12:46Z, f23168 = 2007-04-01T17:22Z, f25000 = 2007-06-19T12:00Z, f29000 = 2007-12-08T12:00Z. Only f23000 and the days around it are in March, so use the date from the table in the UI and the explanation.
- Added by the second agent: Use the table's minute-truncated times for display only. The exact time of file n is 2007-03-25T12:00:00Z + (n-23000) x 3715.2 s, which gives seconds, e.g. 23024 = 12:46:04.8Z.
- Added by the second agent: Hold frames confirmed at the END, not the start, of the 2061-frame comp movie (comp vs m4v frame similarity at the same timestamp is about 25 dB at t = 0, 1, 2, 3, 30 and 66 s, and drops to about 24 dB when offset by 1 s). Clamp k = min(floor(30t), 2000) for 2061- and 2031-frame movies.
