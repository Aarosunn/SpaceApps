## Quick answer

- What it is: Flat world-map movie of modelled monthly wetland methane emissions, Jan 1980 to Dec 2021, 3 frames per month, plus a 114 s narrated cut and sphere versions.
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: LPJ-wsl wetland methane model (Lund-Potsdam-Jena DGVM, Wald Schnee und Landschaft); scientists Lesley Ott and Benjamin Poulter; visualizer Mark SubbaRao (?)
- Period covered: January 1980 to December 2021 (42 years, 504 months)
- Time step of the underlying data: Monthly
- **One frame equals**: One third of a month (about 10 days) on the 4K map movies. 42 x 12 = 504 months; 50.40 s x 30 fps = 1512 frames; 1512 / 504 = 3.0 frames per month exactly. The sphere movie CH4sphere has 900 frames so 504/900 = 0.56 month per frame (not measured).
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: 1980-01
- Date label seen on screen: Bottom-left, two lines: three-letter month over four-digit year (e.g. 'Apr 2016', 'Dec 2021'). Only in ch4_wetlands (labelled) and the narrated MethaneWetalndsFinal; absent in _maponly (description says no date labels). CH4vert2 puts 'Jan 1980' at the top. No time zone (monthly data).
- Update behaviour: Static; rendered once, Dec 2022. mp4/webm Last-Modified 2022-12-01 to 2022-12-10; page record updated 2025-01-06; hwshow 2024-09-03. Not re-rendered on a schedule; newest data month Dec 2021.
- Status on 2026-11-14: static — newest data: 2021-12
- View / camera: ch4_wetlands*: flat world map in a Robinson-like pseudo-cylindrical projection (not equirectangular; curved meridians, elliptical outline), fixed geography and fixed camera, 16:9. Grey land under an inferno-style methane colormap. Separate movies map the data to a rotating sphere (CH4sphere 2160x2160, CH4vert2 1080x1920 vertical with colorbar).
- On-screen clutter: Labelled movie: title 'Methane Emissions from Wetlands', month/year bottom-left, colorbar bottom-right ('grams of methane per square meter per month', ticks 0.0 to 2.0, top arrow), white country borders, and blue region callouts with names (Hudson Bay Lowlands, Western Siberian Lowlands, Amazonia, Pantanal, Curvette Centrale, Sudd Wetlands, Okavango Delta) visible at Jan 2001, Apr 2016, Dec 2021 but absent at Jan 1980 and Jun 1980. Clean variants: ch4_wetlands_nolabels and ch4_wetlands_maponly ('excludes title, date labels, and colorbar'). The grey base-map texture changes with the season (white speckled snow/cloud-like patches in Jun 1980).
- Fit as a sonification source: **excellent** — Real monthly data with a clear annual cycle, a visible 42-year trend and sharp regional contrast (Amazon, Congo, Indonesia, Western Siberia) on a fixed-camera flat map with a printed colour scale (0 to 2 g CH4 per m2 per month); the date can be computed exactly and a clean map-only variant exists.
- Suggested mapping: Convert colour under the cursor to a value via the inferno colorbar (0-2 g/m2/month) and play it as pitch or loudness; get the date from the frame-to-month formula; add the global mean as a slow drone so the Northern Hemisphere summer peak is an audible yearly pulse.

## Gotchas

- Not one frame per day: 3 frames per month; the label changes every 3 frames.
- The narrated MethaneWetalndsFinal.mp4/.webm (114.5 s) has audio and cuts and is NOT a clean time axis; use ch4_wetlands*.
- webm and mp4 probe differ by one frame (1513 vs 1512); unexplained.
- Blue region callouts appear only in the later part of the movie and can be mistaken for data; use the maponly version.
- Grey base-map texture changes monthly (snow/cloud-like) and is not methane.
- Colorbar is clipped at 2.0 with an arrow: higher values saturate to pale yellow.
- All movies carry an audio track (probe audio=True); ignore or mute.

##### Media group 313308 | widget: Basic text with HTML | title: (none)
Description: Methane is an important greenhouse gas that contributes substantially to global warming. On a molecule by molecule basis, methane is much more efficient at trapping heat than carbon dioxide, the main driver of warming. Though human activities, including agriculture, oil and natural gas production and use, and waste disposal, collectively contribute the majority of methane to the atmosphere, about a third of total methane emissions comes from wetlands. Wetland habitats are filled with things like waterlogged soils and permafrost, which makes them sizable carbon sinks. However, as the climate changes, these carbon-rich soils are vulnerable to flooding and to rising temperatures, which can release more carbon to the atmosphere in the form or methane. Understanding methane emissions from natural sources like wetlands is critically important to scientists and policymakers who are working to ensure that changes in natural systems don’t counteract progress in combatting climate change made by reducing emissions from human activities.

This animation shows estimates of wetland methane emissions produced by the Lund–Potsdam–Jena Dynamic Global Vegetation Model (LPJ-DGVM) Wald Schnee und Landscaft version (LPJ-wsl). LPJ-wsl is a prognostic model, meaning that it can be used to simulate future changes in wetland emissions and independently verified with remote sensing data products. The model includes a complex, topography dependent model of near surface hydrology, and a permafrost and dynamic snow model, allowing it to produce realistic distributions of inundated area. Highlighted areas show concentrated methane sources from tropical and high latitude ecosystems. The LPJ-wsl model is regularly used in conjunction with NASA’s GEOS model to simulate the impact of wetlands and other methane sources on atmospheric methane concentrations, compare against satellite and airborne data, and to improve understanding and prediction of wetland emissions.
