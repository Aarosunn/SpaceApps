## Frame → time mapping

No date exists for any frame. The only thing a program can compute is frame index -> scan angle: t = k / 30 s; angle_deg = 12.0 * t = 0.40 * k, counted counterclockwise on screen from 3 o'clock (line pointing right at k = 0), pivot at the centre of the square frame; valid for k = 0 to about 899. From about k = 900 on there is no line. HOW THE SCAN MOVES (measured angles in deg): t=0.01 -> -0.35; 1.0 -> 11.40; 2.0 -> 23.18; 3.0 -> 35.07; 8.54 -> 102.46; 12.81 -> 153.92; 21.34 -> 255.35; 29.87 -> 358.08. The line is present at t=29.87 s and gone at t=30.5 s; the exact frame where it vanishes was not sampled.

## Gotchas

- Not Earth data and not part of the EIC gallery: it is listed only as an official Jukebox resource (bundles/13786.md lines 6-7). Use it as a model and a test image, not as a jukebox track.
- Challenge text vs page text. The challenge's resource blurb (SpaceApps/spaceapps-2026/sources/jukebox.txt line 28) and the team note (02-challenges-2026.md line 70) describe this page as luminosity and spatial coordinates turned into volume and pitch. The NASA page itself states only radius -> pitch, whole image -> choir, and star colour -> instrument; it never mentions brightness or volume. Brightness -> volume is stated on the sibling pages 13997, 13998 and 13999 (svs_api/13786.json lines 192, 211, 230), not here, and I could not confirm it from the audio. Do not cite this page as proof of a luminosity -> volume mapping.
- Page vs files. The media description labels the video a horizontal master version for horizontal display (bundles/13786.md line 34), but both movie files are square, 1920x1920 (ffmpeg probe); only the stills are 16:9. The files win: lay it out as a square.
- No audio-only file. To get the music alone, pull the audio track out of a movie (for example: ffmpeg -i Caldwell73_2K_Final.mp4 -vn -c:a copy caldwell73.m4a). The bundle's audio=True flag does not name the codecs: WebM = Opus 48 kHz stereo, MP4 = AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo 123 kb/s.
- LOCAL BROWSER / CORS. svs.gsfc.nasa.gov answers every request with access-control-allow-origin: https://tempo.multiverse.music, a fixed value that ignores the caller's origin (VERIFIED with Origin http://localhost:8080, Origin null and no Origin header, on the webm, mp4, print.jpg, the SRT and /api/13786). CONSEQUENCE (standard browser CORS rules, not tested in a browser here): a page on localhost can still PLAY the NASA-hosted video in a plain video tag, but it cannot read its pixels through a canvas (tainted canvas), cannot analyse its audio with Web Audio, and cannot fetch() the file or the API JSON. Download the media once and serve it from the app's own origin. Run the app from a local web server (http://localhost), not as a file:// page, because browsers also block canvas readback of file:// media.
- The movie is 34 s but the sonification is 30 s. The line is gone by t=30.5 s; the sound then decays (mid level -23.5 dBFS at 30.0-30.5 s, -41.5 at 31.0-31.5 s, below -60 dBFS after 32.1 s) and the last 2 s are silent. Loop or trim at 30 s, not at the file end.
- Scan direction is not on the page. The text only says the radar scans around; the frames show counterclockwise from 3 o'clock. The sibling NGC 2392 page describes a clockwise scan, so direction is a per-piece choice, not a NASA convention.
- Overlays are burned in for the whole sweep and there is no clean version of the sweeping part; NASA's still print.jpg also has the line in it and is a 16:9 crop.
- The WebM is heavily compressed (1386 kb/s total for 1920x1920): stars are smeared and blocky. Fine for watching and for audio work, poor for per-pixel work; use the MP4 (17.7 Mb/s video) for that.
- Stereo oddity. The left-right balance follows the scan angle in every frequency band, but it is hard right when the line points to about 52 deg (up and to the right, t about 4.4 s) and hard left at about 231 deg (t about 19.3 s), not when the line points straight right or left; it also starts near centre (+0.08) and ends at +0.35. Cause unknown. For our build, pan by screen x instead of copying this.
- Accessibility of the official example is thin: the caption file holds a single cue (0 to 33.6 s) that is only a bracketed two-word tag calling the music pretty, there is no narration (INFERRED from that single cue and from the spectrum; nobody listened), and nothing on screen explains the mapping. A spoken or written legend, real captions and a downloadable audio file would already go beyond it.
- Third-party credit. The sonification is credited to SYSTEM Sounds (M. Russo, A. Santaguida), not to NASA staff. If the clip or its audio appears in the demo, list it in References (team doc 03-rules-and-deadline.md lines 30, 34 and 67: credit all external resources). Reuse terms for the audio are not stated on the page.

#### Page description (verbatim, HTML stripped)
Located 40,000 light-years from Earth in the constellation Columba, this globular cluster is called Caldwell 73 or NGC 1851. A globular cluster is a spherical group of stars that are gravitationally bound to each other, with most of the stars concentrated at the cluster’s center.  As the radar scans around in this sonification, the radius of the stars is mapped to pitch, so stars farther from the center are higher pitched. The entire image is converted to the sound of a choir, while the orange and red stars are represented by a marimba, and the blue stars are represented by a glockenspiel.Credit: SYSTEM Sounds (M. Russo, A. Santaguida)For more information about the Hubble Space Telescope and its images, visit https://nasa.gov/hubble. ||
