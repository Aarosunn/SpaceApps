## Quick answer

- What it is: A single static forest-canopy-height dataset (GEDI lidar on the ISS, about one year of collection) rendered as 3D spikes on a globe: 2 global movies are one slow 360-degree spin, 10 regional movies are a camera fly-in during which the lidar tracks are progressively drawn on. No dates anywhere; a frame index is a camera/animation step, never a calendar day.
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: GEDI (Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation) lidar on the International Space Station; credited dataset 'Relative Height' RH100 = 100th percentile of waveform energy relative to ground elevation, 'Derived from the L2B LIDAR metric RH100 data product' (bundles/4950.md lines 15-16). (observation)
- Period covered: VERIFIED (descriptions disagree): main group 316231 says 'data collected between April 2019 and April 2020' (bundle line 25); all 11 other described groups (316232, 316236-316245) say 'between April 2018 and April 2019' (bundle lines 51, 84, 99, 114, 130, 146, 162, 178, 195, 211, 227). Either way it is ONE roughly 12-month accumulation shown as a single map, not a time series. No file, file name or frame settles which year range is right.
- Time step of the underlying data: None. The underlying data is one time-aggregated map (all lidar shots from the roughly 12-month window merged). There is no daily, monthly or yearly step in any movie or frame set.
- **One frame equals**: GLOBAL movies: for frames 0..2999 one frame = 1/60 s of globe rotation = 0.12 deg of longitude (360/3000), zero real-world time. Verified: frame at t=50.00 s matches t=0.00 s. In GEDI_global_w_colorbar_1080p60 (3089 frames, 51.48 s) the last ~89 frames (t=50.0 to 51.48 s) hold the t=0 pose (t=51.4 matches t=0). REGIONAL movies: one frame = 1/60 s of a camera move plus a track draw-on step; days per frame unknown; camera is static for roughly the first 2 s.
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: Nowhere. File names carry only a render index (GEDI_global_w_colorbar_0000..2998, GEDI_global.0001..2999, GEDI_CA.4000..5400, GEDI_FL_04.5500..6900, GEDI_Goddard_02.7000..8394, GEDI_Laurentides_02.8500..9894, GEDI_senegal.10000..11345). No date is burned into any frame, there is no date-only frame set, dated_sets.json has 0 entries for 4950, and the caption files are 43-byte 'silent' stubs. The only date information is the prose range in the description.
- Date label seen on screen: None. Burned-in text is only the colorbar: title 'Height Above Ground', unit 'meters', tick labels 0 10 20 30 40 50 (global), 0 25 50 75 (California), 0 20 40 (Senegal); Florida, Goddard and Laurentides colorbar tick labels are too small to read in the 1024 px stills (Florida and Goddard appear to end at 50). The California movie also shows base-map state names (WASHINGTON, OREGON, IDAHO, MONTANA, WYOMING, NEVADA, UTAH, COLORADO, CALIFORNIA, ARIZONA, NORTH DAKOTA, SOUTH DAKOTA) at the start, which get covered as data is drawn. No NASA insignia, no title, no date, no time zone in any sampled frame.
- Update behaviour: Static, rendered once per scene and never re-rendered on a schedule. Global and California files: Last-Modified 26-27 Oct 2021 (frame files Oct 26, 2021). Florida, Goddard, Laurentides: frames Apr 15 and Apr 18, 2022, movies 18-19 Apr 2022 (Goddard w_colorbar and Laurentides_02 movies re-encoded 8-9 Jul 2022). Senegal and the 0-40 m colorbar: 22-23 Sep 2022. Caption stubs 23 Aug 2022. The page record 'last updated 2025-04-01' is a metadata edit; no media file is newer than 23 Sep 2022.
- Status on 2026-11-14: static — newest data: April 2020 according to the main description, April 2019 according to the other 11 descriptions; no day-level date exists.
- View / camera: Global: lit 3D globe, uniform spin, 360 deg in 50.00 s, then ~1.5 s hold (w_colorbar only). Regional: oblique view of base map with static camera for about 2 s, then tilting toward the horizon; geography is NOT moving in every frame (first ~2 s are fixed). Pixel position never maps to fixed lat/lon during global spin.
- On-screen clutter: Low. w_colorbar variants carry one small colorbar block (bottom-left; bottom-right in the Goddard still). The California opening frames carry state-name labels and state borders on the base map. Otherwise nothing: no dates, logos or insets. Main difficulty is not clutter but 3D shading, perspective and spike occlusion.
- Clean variant: Yes. Every scene has a no-colorbar version: GEDI_global (2160p60 mp4/webm, prores, 2999 TIFF), GEDI_CA (2160p60 mp4/webm, prores, 1401 TIFF), GEDI_FL, GEDI_Goddard(_02), GEDI_Laurentides_02 (1080p60 + 2160p, EXR frames), GEDI_senegal (1080p60 + 2160p, TIFF). Standalone colorbar stills exist for 0-50 m, 0-75 m and 0-40 m (print.jpg 1024x576 and 3840x2160 TIFF). INFERRED from byte counts, not opened: the 33,195,034-byte TIFFs = 3840*2160*4 bytes (33,177,600) + header, so 8-bit 4-channel; the 66,355,478-byte TIFFs = 3840*2160*8 (66,355,200) + header, so 16-bit 4-channel; a 4th channel suggests alpha. No date layers exist because there are no dates.
- What the colours mean: Color AND spike length both encode canopy height above ground (RH100) in meters: brown and dark green = short vegetation, bright green to white = tall; the colorbar has an arrow tip on the high end (values above the top tick saturate to white). The scale differs per scene: 0-50 m global, 0-75 m California, 0-40 m Senegal. Blue is ocean, grey-white at the poles/horizon is the base map where GEDI has no coverage (ISS orbit limits coverage to about 51.6 degrees N/S - background knowledge, not from the evidence). Bare tan/beige patches between tracks in regional views are base map with no lidar shot, not zero height. Lighting darkens colors on the night side of the globe.
- Fit as a sonification source: **fair** — The picture is beautiful and the challenge text names it, but there is no time axis to play: the data is one static map, so 'play the days' is impossible here. What can be sonified is SPACE: the 50 s global spin is one lap around the Earth, and height can be read from color. Accuracy is limited because the frames are a lit 3D render (shading, perspective, spikes occluding each other, night side darker), not a flat georeferenced map, and each regional movie uses a different color scale.
- Suggested mapping: Use the clean global spin as a 50-second 'lap around the planet'. For each sampled frame (for example every 30th frame = 100 steps of 3.6 degrees longitude) read a vertical strip of pixels down the center of the globe disc (the meridian facing the camera, which is least distorted and fully lit). Classify each pixel: blue = ocean, brown-to-white = canopy height via a lookup table built from the 0-50 m colorbar still. Map mean canopy height in the strip to pitch (taller forest = higher), land fraction to loudness (open ocean = quiet wave pad), and split the strip into north / tropics / south bands played as three voices or panned left-centre-right. Announce region names by speech as they pass (Pacific, Asia, Africa, Atlantic, Americas) for blind users. The regional draw-on clips can add a second scene: count of non-base-map pixels per frame -> a rising density of 'laser tick' sounds.

## Frame → time mapping

No frame-to-date mapping. Global (60 fps): sub-camera longitude = lon0 - 0.12*k deg for k in 0..2999 (lap exactly 50.00 s, verified); for k >= 3000 (t >= 50.0 s, only in the w_colorbar movies) the view is frozen at the k=0 pose. t=49.07 s is NOT almost identical to t=0: it is 0.93 s*7.2 = 6.7 deg short (Australia about 40 px further left in f4_t49.07 than in f0_t0.00). Regional (CA): frames 0-60 base map with no data and static camera (t=0 and t=1.0 views identical); between t=1.0 and t=2.0 s a dense crosshatch of tracks appears (t=2.0 is already a dense grid, not 'sparse'); camera starts tilting after about 2-3 s; by t=21.33 s full coverage and low horizon view. Draw-on is not known to be linear in date.

## Gotchas

- There is NO time series on this page. Global movies = static data + rotating globe. Do not label any frame with a date.
- The cluster note 'camera fly-throughs of static data' is only fully true for the 2 global groups. The 10 regional groups also animate the data being drawn on ('Data is drawn on the globe over time, depicting how data coverage increases with each satellite pass'), verified in frames; this is an ordering of coverage with no dates and no known days-per-frame.
- NASA's own description contradicts itself on the collection period: 'April 2019 and April 2020' (main group) versus 'April 2018 and April 2019' (11 other groups). Files cannot settle it. Background knowledge (not in the evidence): GEDI was launched to the ISS in December 2018, so a start of April 2018 cannot be right; quote the range as 'about one year starting April 2019' and flag it as NASA-described.
- Color scale differs by scene: 0-50 m global, 0-75 m California, 0-40 m Senegal. One lookup table does not fit all movies.
- Pixel color is not a pure data value: 3D spikes, directional lighting (one limb of the globe in shadow), perspective and JPEG/VP8 compression all shift colors. Expect rough height classes (3-5 levels), not meters.
- Tan/beige gaps between tracks in regional views are 'no lidar shot here' (base map), not low vegetation. Grey-white polar areas are outside GEDI coverage.
- Movie frame counts do not always equal frame-set counts: w_colorbar 1080p60 movies are about 90 frames longer (global 3089 vs 2999 files; FL 1491 vs 1401; Goddard 1485 vs 1395; Laurentides 1486 vs 1396) and the 2160p60 ones about 30 longer (3029, 1431, 1425, 1426). Movie frame index is therefore not the frame-set index for those files.
- The page's item counts are one higher than the directory listings for every frame set (3000 vs 2999, 1402 vs 1401, 1396 vs 1395, 1397 vs 1396, 1347 vs 1346); the listings match the numeric ranges in the file names.
- Frame sets are unusable in a browser and far too big for a hackathon: 4K TIFF at 31.7 or 63.3 MB per frame (44 to 190 GB per set) and EXR for FL, Goddard, Laurentides clean sets. Use the WebM/MP4 movies.
- Frame-set numbering is a render-timeline index with offsets (CA starts at 4000, FL 5500, Goddard 7000, Laurentides 8500, Senegal 10000); the numbers are not dates or orbit numbers.
- All movies are 60 fps (or 30 fps for *_2160p30) and silent (audio=False); the .srt files are 43-byte 'silent' caption stubs, so there is no narration or description text to reuse for accessibility.
- ProRes .mov files (3.3 GB and 4.0 GB) are not browser-playable; ignore them.
- If the team wants real numbers rather than colors, the actual GEDI L2B RH100 data would have to come from outside this page (https://gedi.umd.edu/ is the only pointer given); nothing downloadable here is numeric data.

#### Page description (verbatim, HTML stripped)

##### Media group 316230 | widget: Basic text with HTML | title: (none)
Description: The Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation (GEDI) produces high resolution laser ranging observations of the 3D structure of the Earth. GEDI’s precise measurements of forest canopy height, canopy vertical structure, and surface elevation greatly advance our ability to characterize important carbon and water cycling processes, biodiversity, and habitat.
GEDI’s data on surface structure are also of immense value for weather forecasting, forest management, glacier and snowpack monitoring, and the generation of more accurate digital elevation models. GEDI provides the missing piece – 3D structure – in NASA’s observational assets which enables us to better understand how the Earth behaves as a system, and guides the actions we can take to sustain critical resources.
The GEDI instrument is a geodetic-class, light detection and ranging (lidar) laser system comprised of 3 lasers that produce 8 parallel tracks of observations. Each laser fires 242 times per second and illuminates a 25 m spot (a footprint) on the surface over which 3D structure is measured. Each footprint is separated by 60 m along track, with an across-track distance of about 600 m between each of the 8 tracks. GEDI expected to produce about 10 billion cloud-free observations during its nominal 24-month mission length.
To learn more, visit the GEDI webpage
