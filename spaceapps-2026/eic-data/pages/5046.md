## Quick answer

- What it is: Fixed-camera globe view of the Arctic with one rendered frame per day of AMSR2 sea ice: complete calendar-year sets for 2021-2024 plus a rolling 730-file (~two-year) set whose mp4 was rewritten today but whose newest data is stuck at 2026-10-30.
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: JAXA AMSR2 (GCOM-W1 SHIZUKU) 10 km daily sea ice concentration + 10 km daily 89 GHz brightness temperature (observed), drawn over MODIS Blue Marble Next Generation monthly land imagery. NASA text: running 3-day minimum, minimum threshold concentration 15% (bundles/5046.md lines 15-17, 29). (observation)
- Period covered: VERIFIED (dated_sets.json + live listing re-fetched 2026-11-14 ~19:37 UTC). Per-year sets: 2021, 2022, 2023 (365 files each, Jan 1 - Dec 31, 0 missing) and 2024 (366 files, 2024-01-01..2024-12-31). There is NO 2025 per-year set. Rolling two-year set/movie: 2024-10-30 .. 2026-10-30 = 731-day inclusive span holding 730 files (1 day missing: 2026-07-27).
- Time step of the underlying data: 1 day (JAXA daily product; one rendered frame per day). NASA says a running 3-day minimum is applied but does NOT say whether that window ends on, is centred on, or starts at the labelled day: window alignment unknown. Land background is monthly Blue Marble cross-faded.
- **One frame equals**: 1 calendar day. Arithmetic re-done and frames opened by the verifier (two-year mp4, 30 fps, 24.33 s x 30 = 730 frames): frame 0 label 'Oct 30 2024'; frame 240 label 'Jun 27 2025' (2024-10-30 + 240 d: 62 d to Dec 31 2024, 240-62 = day-of-year 178 of 2025 = Jun 27) -> 240 days / 240 frames = 1 day per frame; frame 480 label 'Feb 22 2026' (480-62-365 = day 53 = Feb 22); last frame 729 label 'Oct 30 2026' = 730 days after frame 0, i.e. 249 frames cover 250 days between frame 480 and the end, so exactly one day is skipped (the folder listing says it is 2026-07-27). 2024 movie: 12.20 s x 30 = 366 frames; last frame opened reads 'Dec 31 2024'. Playback: 30 days of data per second.

- Fit as a sonification source: **good** — One frame = one day, fixed camera, no colorbar or legend, a clean no-date variant, and a strong smooth annual cycle that maps naturally to sound. Marked down from excellent because the picture is a shaded rendering, not calibrated data (white is also snow on land and Greenland), the view is a globe so pixel counts are not true area, and the 'live' two-year file is frozen at 2026-10-30.
- Suggested mapping: Build a static ocean mask once (pixels that are ocean-blue in a September frame), then per frame count white pixels inside the mask = ice-extent proxy. Map extent to pitch of a sustained drone, day-to-day change to a percussive density (freeze = soft ticks, melt = dripping notes), and announce month boundaries by speech from the computed date. Optional: split the disc into 6-8 angular sectors and give each its own voice/pan.

Description: This visualization shows the daily Arctic sea ice and seasonal land cover change progressing through time, with a single frame rendered for each day (available from the drop-down of each image window), and an animation created from these frames. 
The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) provides many water-related products derived from data acquired by the Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer 2 (AMSR2) instrument aboard the Global Change Observation Mission 1st-Water "SHIZUKU" (GCOM-W1) satellite. Two JAXA datasets are used in this animation:  the 10-km daily sea ice concentration and the 10 km daily 89 GHz Brightness Temperature.
In this visualization sea ice changes from day to day, with the amount of ice shown being determined by the AMSR2 sea ice concentration data. A running 3-day minimum is used, with a minimum threshhold concentration of 15%. The blueish white color of the sea ice is derived from a 3-day running minimum of the AMSR2 89 GHz brightness temperature. Over the terrain, monthly data from the seasonal Blue Marble Next Generation fades slowly from month to month.
The numerical portion of the frame filename begins with the four-digit year, followed by the three-digit day of the year for that frame.
