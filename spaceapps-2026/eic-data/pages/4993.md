## Quick answer

- What it is: Static 2022 render: a 3D oblique terrain view of the 2021 Dixie Fire (California) growing from Jul 14 to Oct 22 2021 with a burned-in date label and a cumulative burned-area graph; time runs at two different speeds, so it is NOT one frame per day.
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: VIIRS active fire detections (Suomi-NPP) processed by an object-based fire tracking system (Chen et al. 2022, Sci Data 9, 249; dataset date_range 07/14/2021 - 10/22/2021), drawn over Landsat-8 OLI band 4-3-2 imagery of 7/11/2021 draped on SRTM terrain (bundles/4993.md lines 15-17). (observation)
- Period covered: Fire data Jul 14 2021 to Oct 22 2021 = 101 days (18 days Jul 14-31 + 31 Aug + 30 Sep + 22 Oct), matching the graph x-axis 'Days 1 ... 101'. The burned-in label starts one day earlier at 'Jul 13 2021' (pre-fire intro hold) and ends at 'Oct 22 2021'.
- Time step of the underlying data: 12 hours (per NASA description: perimeter and fire lines updated every 12 hours from new VIIRS detections) = 202 steps over 101 days. The on-screen label shows the date only, so the 12-hour half-steps are not labelled.
- **One frame equals**: Variable. 60 fps master / 6136-file EXR set: about 20 minutes of fire time per frame from Jul 14 to about Sep 11 (about 71.7 frames per day, about 36 frames per 12-hour step), then exactly 1 hour per frame from about Sep 12 to Oct 22 (24 frames per day, 12 frames per 12-hour step). Dixie_fire_2021_p30_1080p30.mp4 (every 2nd master frame): about 40 min then 2 h per frame (about 18 then 6 frames per 12-hour step). Dixie_fire_2021_4x_1080p30.mp4 (every 4th master frame): about 80 min then 4 h per frame (about 9 then 3 frames per 12-hour step). The first about 244 master frames (intro, label 'Jul 13 2021') and the last about 628 master frames (label frozen at 'Oct 22 2021', colour-coded recap) do not advance the date.
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: Only from the label burned into the picture (top-left dark box, white text, format 'Mon DD YYYY', e.g. 'Aug 22 2021'; needs OCR or template matching; fixed position about x 28-210, y 25-72 on a 1280x720 scale) or from the fitted piecewise formula in frame_to_time_mapping. File names carry only a render index (Dixie_fire_2021.1000.exr ... .7135.exr), no date. There is no separate date-only frame set, and the page is absent from dated_sets.json.
- Date label seen on screen: Yes: date only ('Jul 13 2021' ... 'Oct 22 2021'), no time of day, no time zone. Also on screen: graph panel titled 'Area' with y-axis 'km2' ticks 300 to 3600 in steps of 300 and x-axis 'Days' ticks 1, 50, 101.
- Update behaviour: Static, rendered once. EXR frames modified Apr 12-18 2022; main movies Last-Modified Apr 18-19 2022 (e.g. Dixie_fire_2021_p30_1080p30.mp4 Mon, 18 Apr 2022 21:19:47 GMT); the two *_p30_*p60 movies May 16 2022; locator-map PNGs Jun 1 2022; Dixie_fire_2021.7135.png May 15 2023. Page record edit 2025-06-23 is metadata only. No rolling window.
- Status on 2026-11-14: static — newest data: 2021-10-22
- View / camera: 3D oblique perspective view of shaded terrain (not a flat map). The camera moves: it starts low over Chico/Paradise looking toward Lake Almanor (Jul 13), is closer in on the Jul 21 and Jul 31 frames, and is at a wider framing that looks identical in the sampled frames at Aug 22, Sep 13 and Oct 22 (Lake Almanor label at about x=490, y=310 of 1280x720 in all three). Geography therefore has no fixed pixel position before about Aug 22. The graph panel and date label are fixed screen overlays in every frame.
- On-screen clutter: Date box top-left; right-hand translucent graph panel (about x 1050-1260, y 90-695 on 1280x720) with 'Area', 'km2', 300..3600, 'Days', 1/50/101; a scale bar ('miles' 0-5, 'kilometers' 0-8) that moves with the camera; white place names (Chico, Durham, Paradise, Magalia, Belden, Twain, Paxton, Quincy, Greenville, Crescent Mills, Taylorsville, Canyondam, Chester, Westwood, Susanville, Lake Almanor). No NASA insignia, no colorbar, no legend.
- Clean variant: No clean variant found in the bundle (no label-free, graph-free or alpha layer files). The page description mentions 'Dixie_fire_animation_only_2021_1080p60.hwshow' but no such file is in the file list; unknown whether the EXR frames carry extra layers.
- What the colours mean: No colorbar. Grey draped area = estimated total area burned so far. Yellow/orange outlines = active fire lines of the last 60 hours (brightest yellow = newest front). Red dots = VIIRS active fire detections. Graph: grey fill = cumulative burned area in km2 versus day number, orange vertical line = current day. In the final recap (label frozen at Oct 22 2021) every 12-hour fire line is redrawn colour-coded by time, orange (earliest) through red, magenta, purple to blue (latest), and the graph fill is recoloured the same way up to about day 60. Background = Landsat-8 natural colour on SRTM relief.
- Fit as a sonification source: **good** — A single clear story with a monotonic numeric signal that is easy to read from pixels: the cumulative-area graph and the date label sit at fixed screen positions in every frame, and the red detection dots give a second 'activity' signal. Held back from excellent because the camera moves (map pixels are not fixed geography before about Aug 22), time runs at two speeds (3x faster after about Sep 11), the smallest movie is 97 MB, and the page is not in the official Jukebox resource list (bundle line 7).
- Suggested mapping: Sample one frame per fire day using the piecewise formula (confirm with OCR of the date box). Read the height of the grey fill / orange marker in the fixed graph panel -> cumulative burned area 0-3600 km2 -> pitch of a sustained tone (rising as the fire grows). Day-to-day increase in that height -> loudness of a low rumble (fast-spread days are loud). Count of saturated red pixels outside the graph panel -> density of crackle/click grains (active detections). One soft tick per day and a spoken date once a week ('July twenty-first') for blind users; announce the end of spread when the curve goes flat (mid-September). The final recap can be played as an orange-to-blue arpeggio.

## Gotchas

- Not one frame per day and not constant speed: about 71.7 master frames per day until about Sep 11, then 24 per day (3x faster). A linear index-to-date formula over the whole movie is wrong by weeks (e.g. the p30 movie is at Aug 22, day 40 of 101, at 50% of its duration).
- First ~4 s of the full-speed movies (about 244 master frames) show 'Jul 13 2021' with no fire, although the description says the period starts July 14.
- Last ~10 s of the full-speed movies (about 628 master frames; from t ~ 45.9 s in the 4x movie) keep the label 'Oct 22 2021' while the fire lines are replayed colour-coded and the graph is recoloured; these frames are a recap, not new dates.
- Movie naming is confusing: 'p30' = every 2nd frame of the 60 fps master; '4x' = every 4th frame. Dixie_fire_2021_1080p60.mp4 (6137 frames, 102.28 s) and Dixie_fire_2021_p30_1080p30.mp4 (3068 frames, 102.27 s) run at the same real speed; Dixie_fire_2021_4x_1080p30.mp4 (1534 frames, 51.13 s) and Dixie_fire_2021_p30_1080p60.mp4 (3069 frames, 51.15 s) are double speed.
- Description vs files: page text lists '3840x2160_16x9_60p [0 Item(s)]' and item counts of 6137 / 1535 / 3071, while the directory listings have 6136 / 1534 / 3070 files (the 30p folder includes two empty stray files named 'a' and 'b', leaving 3068 EXR). 'Coming soon to our YouTube channel' is stale text from 2022.
- Frame sets are EXR only (36 MB per 4K frame, 55 to 220 GB per set): not browser-decodable and far too large; there are no PNG/JPEG frame sets.
- The date label has no time of day, so the two 12-hour steps inside one date cannot be told apart from the label; whether the perimeter jumps every 12 hours or is interpolated between steps was not checked.
- The scale bar and place names move with the camera, and the graph panel is translucent (terrain shows through), so threshold on the grey fill / orange line rather than exact colours.
- Movies are silent (audio=False); captions file is a 43-byte 'silent' placeholder.
- Media group 314539 is a different kind of picture: a flat locator map of California ('Dixie Wildfire 2021', legend Fire Perimeter / National Park / Populated Area / Forested Area, inset of North America), perimeter as of Oct 22 2021; PNG at 1920x1080 (2.3 MB), 3840x2160 (8.2 MB), 16000x9000 (17.7 MB).

##### Media group 314532 | widget: Basic text with HTML | title: (none)
Description: This visualization highlights data from a new fire detection and tracking approach (Chen et al., 2022) based on near-real time active fire detections from the VIIRS sensor on the Suomi-NPP satellite. Every 12 hours, the fire tracking algorithm uses new active fire detections to update the total fire perimeter and estimate the position of active fire lines where the fire may continue to spread.  Yellow lines indicate the new fire fronts from active fire data (red points) every 12 hours. This approach provides a detailed perspective on the behavior of the Dixie fire, the largest fire in California history.  The fire tracking data identify periods of rapid fire expansion, spot fires from blowing embers outside of the large fire perimeter, and active fire detections within the perimeter from continued flaming and smoldering behind the active fire fronts.  In total, the Dixie fire burned for more than 100 days, including more than a month of fire activity after the perimeter was contained in mid-September.
