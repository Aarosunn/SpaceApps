- Fit as a sonification source: **good** — One frame = one day, fixed camera, no clutter, clean variant, current (2-day lag today), and the Antarctic signal is the simplest in the cluster: a white ring around a constant white continent that is wide in the Jul/Aug/Nov frames and nearly gone in the Mar 04 frame. - Suggested mapping: Mask the continent using a late-February / early-March frame, count remaining white pixels per frame = ice-extent proxy. Map extent to the loudness and bandwidth of a wind-like pad and scan the ring by angle around the pole so each of ~12 sectors plays one note whose pitch follows that sector's ice-edge radius. Speak the computed date at each month change.

## Gotchas

- Page-record update date (2026-01-09) is misleading: the files show this page is updating (newest frame written Nov 14 2026).
- 'Updating daily' means nightly when healthy: 132 of the 304 files written after Jan 9 2026 arrived in daytime batches (70 on May 1 2026 alone), and Oct 28 - Nov 1 arrived together on Nov 2. Pin a local copy for the demo.
- DESCRIPTION vs FILES: page item counts are one too high ('731' vs 730 files; '367' vs 366; '366' vs 365).
- Rolling window is 730 files, not two calendar years: today it spans 734 days with 4 missing days (2026-09-22, 2026-10-14, 2026-11-06, 2026-11-07). A naive start + index formula is wrong by up to 4 days at the end.
- The bundle's 'step 635 / 636' are year rollovers in the YYYYDDD numbering, not gaps; the real gaps are the two 'step 2' entries plus one step of 3 (the bundle's step summary lists only the four most common steps).
- Stale .webm siblings: two-year .webm from 10 Apr 2023; antarctic_sea_ice_2023_w_dates.webm and antarctic_sea_ice_2023.webm have only ~64 frames.
- antarctic_sea_ice_2023_no_dates.mp4 probes as ~367 frames (12.23 s) vs 365 files in its folder: frame -> date for that file is unknown.
- No per-year 2025 folder or movie; 2025 lives only in the rolling window.
- Page top-level description is the placeholder 'Summary'.
- EXR frames (63 MB with dates, ~6 MB without) are not browser-decodable and too big to bulk download.
- CORS (verified by HEAD with an Origin header on this page's mp4): 'access-control-allow-origin: https://tempo.multiverse.music'. INFERRED consequence: canvas pixel reads of a cross-origin <video> are blocked; self-host the downloaded mp4.
- The Antarctic continent is white in every frame - without a mask the 'white pixel' signal never drops below a large constant.

Description: This visualization shows the daily Antarctic sea ice and seasonal land cover change progressing through time, with a single frame rendered for each day (available from the drop-down of each image window), and an animation created from these frames. 
The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) provides many water-related products derived from data acquired by the Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer 2 (AMSR2) instrument aboard the Global Change Observation Mission 1st-Water "SHIZUKU" (GCOM-W1) satellite. Two JAXA datasets are used in this animation:  the 10-km daily sea ice concentration and the 10 km daily 89 GHz Brightness Temperature.
In this visualization sea ice changes from day to day, with the amount of ice shown being determined by the AMSR2 sea ice concentration data. A running 3-day minimum is used, with a minimum threshhold concentration of 15%. The blueish white color of the sea ice is derived from a 3-day running minimum of the AMSR2 89 GHz brightness temperature. Over the terrain, monthly data from the seasonal Blue Marble Next Generation fades slowly from month to month.
