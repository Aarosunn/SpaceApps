- Data source: JAXA AMSR2 (GCOM-W1 SHIZUKU) 10 km daily sea ice concentration + 10 km daily 89 GHz brightness temperature (observed), over MODIS Blue Marble Next Generation monthly imagery; running 3-day minimum, threshold 15% (bundles/5099.md lines 15-17, 43). (observation)

- What the colours mean: Not a quantitative colormap. White / blue-white over ocean = sea ice (3-day-minimum concentration at least 15%, texture from 89 GHz brightness temperature); flat blue = open ocean; land is true-colour Blue Marble by month (Arctic land green in the Jul/Aug frames, snow-white in November); Greenland and Antarctica are white in every frame opened. A per-hemisphere land/ice-sheet mask is needed to isolate sea ice.
- Fit as a sonification source: **excellent** — One frame = one day with dates recoverable from file names, fixed cameras, a text-free variant, data current to 2 days today, and two hemispheres in one frame whose ice cycles run in opposite phase (in the 05 Aug 2026 frame the Arctic pack is small while the Antarctic ring is wide) - a ready-made left/right stereo duet a blind listener can follow without any legend. Caveats: rendered picture not calibrated values, globe distortion, white land/ice-sheet needs masking, 4 skipped days, and the feed has a record of stalls.
- Suggested mapping: Split the frame at x = width/2. For each half build a static mask of always-white pixels (Greenland / Antarctica) and land, then count white ocean pixels per frame = ice-extent proxy per pole. Arctic -> left-ear voice, Antarctic -> right-ear voice; extent drives pitch, daily change drives a soft tick rate (freeze) or falling droplets (melt). Speak the month/year from the computed date; let the user scrub by day with arrow keys and hear a spoken summary per pole.

## Gotchas

- Page-record update (2026-10-24) is not the data date: the movie was rewritten today; newest data today is 2026-11-12.
- DESCRIPTION vs FILES: the top-level description lists the .mp4 as 39.5 MB and the frame set as 'w_dates (4320x2160) [0 Item(s)]', the media group says '731 item(s)'; the real folder has 730 files and the mp4 is 40.5 MB today (size changes as it is rewritten).
- File names end in '_h265.mp4' but the codec is h264 (High profile) - good news for browsers; do not filter it out as HEVC.
- Movie resolution (4096x2048) differs from frame resolution (4320x2160): masks made on one do not transfer 1:1 to the other.
- Both .webm copies are from 12 May 2023; the pre-extracted frames in frames/5099/g312530 and g312531 come from these stale files - use fresh_g312530 / fresh_g312531 / extra_verify.
- Rolling window = 730 files, not 730 consecutive days: today it spans 734 days with 2026-09-22, 2026-10-14, 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-07 missing. A naive 'start + index' date is wrong by 1 to 4 days near the end.
- This page's gaps follow the Antarctic set (5064), not the Arctic set (5046): it has 2026-07-27, which 5046 lacks, so it is its own render and not a paste of the two other folders.
- The bundle's 'step 635 / 636' are year rollovers in YYYYDDD numbering, not gaps.
- Date label order is 'DD Mon YYYY' here but 'Mon DD YYYY' on 5046/5064.
- CORS (verified by HEAD with an Origin header on both mp4s): 'access-control-allow-origin: https://tempo.multiverse.music'. Description: This visualization shows the daily Arctic and Antarctic sea ice and seasonal land cover change over a two-year history from the present, with a single frame rendered for each day (available from the drop-down of each image window), and an animation created from these frames, 
The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) provides many water-related products derived from data acquired by the Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer 2 (AMSR2) instrument aboard the Global Change Observation Mission 1st-Water "SHIZUKU" (GCOM-W1) satellite. Two JAXA datasets are used in this animation: the 10-km daily sea ice concentration and the 10 km daily 89 GHz Brightness Temperature.
In this visualization sea ice changes from day to day, with the amount of ice shown being determined by the AMSR2 sea ice concentration data. A running 3-day minimum is used, with a minimum threshhold concentration of 15%. The blueish white color of the sea ice is derived from a 3-day running minimum of the AMSR2 89 GHz brightness temperature. Over the terrain, monthly data from the seasonal Blue Marble Next Generation fades slowly from month to month.
