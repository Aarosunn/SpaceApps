## Quick answer

- What it is: 3D rendered globe with cyan orbit trails and name labels of about 30 NASA Earth-observing satellites over a 3-hour window; it shows where satellites are, not any measurement of Earth, and the clouds are from an unrelated model date.
- Kind: data-animation
- Data source: CelesTrak two-line element sets (TLE orbit ephemeris) propagated to satellite positions; cloud layer is a GEOS model run from a different, unstated period (page text: 'the clouds shown do not represent actual conditions for today'). No Earth-science measurement is displayed. (model)
- Period covered: VERIFIED from frames of the current movie: Nov 13 2026 16:48 to Nov 13 2026 19:48 (UTC per page description; the label itself has no time-zone text) = exactly 3 hours. The window moves each time the movie is re-rendered.
- Time step of the underlying data: Orbit positions are computed analytically from TLEs per rendered frame (6 s of simulated time per frame). Cloud texture: GEOS at 10-minute steps interpolated per frame (page text), not for the labelled date.
- **One frame equals**: 6 seconds of simulated time. Arithmetic: 19:48 - 16:48 = 180 min = 10800 s; frames 0..1800 = 1800 intervals; 10800 / 1800 = 6.0 s per frame. Cross-checks: frame 540 shows 17:42 (540 x 6 s = 54 min after 16:48, matches); frame 1140 shows 18:42 (1140 x 6 s = 114 min, matches). At 60 fps the movie runs 360x real time (1 s of video = 6 min).
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: Only the label burned into the picture (needs OCR or a manual read of frame 0), then the formula above. File names carry no date (fleet_today_30sec*.mp4; frames are plain indexes 00000..01800). HTTP Last-Modified gives the render time, which is NOT the simulated time (render landed 14.9 h before the first simulated minute).
- Date label seen on screen: White text, format 'Nov 13  2026  16:48' (month day year HH:MM, minutes resolution, no seconds, no 'UTC'). Bottom-left in the 16:9 versions (about x 6-18%, y 90% of frame), bottom-right in the square version.
- Update behaviour: Movies are overwritten in place at a fixed URL, whole file each time (3 separate renders about 25 min apart). - Status on 2026-11-14: claims-daily-but-stale — newest data: Nov 13 2026 19:48 UTC (last frame of the current movie; simulated orbit time, not an observation time)
- View / camera: Perspective 3D globe seen from space, not a flat map. Geography moves: the Americas shift by roughly 45 degrees of longitude between first and last frame (consistent with 3 h x 15 deg/h Earth rotation; inference from frames) and the framing/tilt changes slightly, so a pixel has no fixed lat/lon. Night side is black on the right.
- On-screen clutter: About 25-30 cyan satellite name labels with cyan trail arcs that overlap each other and the globe (labels read on the sampled frames: SWOT, SENTINEL-6B, SMAP, GPM-CORE, Landsat 8, Landsat 9, GRACE-FO-1, GRACE-FO-2, CYGNSS-1/2/3/4/5/7/8, Suomi NPP, Aqua, Aura, Terra, ICESat-2, NISAR, PACE, OCO-2, ISS). White date/time label. Star field in the main version. No title, no colorbar, no legend, no NASA insignia.
- Clean variant: Partly: _nostars (black background, 16:9) and _nostars_sq (black background, 2160x2160). No variant without labels or without the date. The EXR frame sets might carry alpha but are 10-69 GB, not browser-decodable and a year stale.
- What the colours mean: No data colormap. Natural-colour Earth texture with white model clouds (not today's weather); cyan = satellite trails and labels; white = clock.
- Fit as a sonification source: **poor** — It is not a visualization of Earth change: the only real data are satellite positions, drawn as overlapping text labels and thin arcs on a rotating 3D globe, and the clouds are admitted by NASA to be from a different date. Pixel values carry no measurable quantity, and it spans 3 hours, not days. Anything meaningful (which satellite is over which place) is far easier to compute from TLEs than to recover from pixels.
- Suggested mapping: If used at all, as an intro/ident rather than a data track: one soft voice per satellite, triggered when its cyan label crosses the top or bottom limb of the globe (polar passes), with the clock label read out by speech at start and end ('16:48 to 19:48 UTC'). A real version would skip the pixels and drive the voices from CelesTrak TLE positions.

## Gotchas

- Not Earth-change data and not listed in the official Jukebox resource list (bundle: 'Named in the official Jukebox challenge resource list: no').
- Clouds are fake for the date (page text): never describe them as today's weather.
- 'Now' / 'today' is loose: the file rendered at 01:52 UTC Nov 13 shows 16:48-19:48 UTC Nov 13, i.e. a propagated window 14.9 h AFTER the render; and on Nov 14 at 19:28 UTC it was still the Nov 13 file.
- Page says the still image 'is updated every 10 minutes'; the file fleet_now_print.jpg has Last-Modified Wed 11 Jun 2025 14:00:04 GMT and its label reads 'Jun 11 2025 14:00'. Description is out of date; files win.
- Frame sets are EXR (not browser-decodable), 10.6-68.7 GB, and all dated Nov 24, 2025: they do not match the current movie. Page says 1802 items, listing has 1801.
- Satellite list on the page and the stale still differ (still alt text lists CALIPSO and Cloudsat; current list has NISAR, PACE, Sentinel-6B, TROPICS).
- Media group 313065 is labelled 3840x2160 on the page but the movie is 2160x2160 (ffmpeg probe).
- Globe rotates and labels overlap, so there is no stable pixel-to-place mapping; time label has minute resolution only.
- Cross-origin pixel reading: a HEAD on this server (tested on the 5577 movie) returned 'access-control-allow-origin: https://tempo.multiverse.music', so a page on another origin can play the video but cannot read its pixels from a canvas without a proxy or a local copy. Not tested on the 5067 files themselves.
- Added by the second agent: 5067 labels have minute resolution only, so 6 s/frame is derived from the 0/540/1140/1800 labels (+-1 min); never claim a per-frame second. At 20:20 UTC Sat Nov 14 the movie was still the Nov 13 render, and its window 16:48-19:48 UTC Nov 13 was already over.
