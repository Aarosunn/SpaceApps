## Quick answer

- What it is: Seasonal-cycle line chart: one line per year (Jan-Dec on x, about -3 to +3 degC on y), years accumulate 1880 to 2024 and cool-blue lines turn red.
- Kind: chart-or-graph-animation
- Data source: GISTEMP v4 monthly anomalies (baseline 1951-1980) plus MERRA-2 seasonal offsets (NASA page text) (?)
- Period covered: 1880 to 2024, one line per calendar year; on-screen year label 1880 at t=0 s, 2024 at t=29 s
- Time step of the underlying data: monthly (12 points per year line)
- **One frame equals**: About 1.2 months of one year's line being drawn (approx 9.8 frames per year, inferred from labels). Not a day. Movie 60 fps, ~1799-1801 frames, 30 s.
- **One frame = one day?**: no
- Where a program gets a frame's date: 1880 (year label only; no month/day labels except the JAN..DEC axis)
- Date label seen on screen: Yes: big year number mid-screen (1880, 1941, 1997, 2024 seen), white then tinted to the current line colour (pink-red at 2024). Month names JAN-DEC on x axis. Y labels +3C ... -3C (green gridlines). No NASA logo visible in the sampled frames.
- Update behaviour: Re-rendered on a schedule in the past: page text says 'updated roughly two weeks after the end of each month'. mp4 degC Last-Modified Wed 08 Jan 2025 22:31:24 GMT, degF 22:55:32 GMT, Spanish 22:32:16 GMT; EXR frames first/last Jan 9 2025 11:18 AM (tz unknown) but folder also holds files dated Jan 9/10 2024 (stale frames mixed in); searchweb.png Last-Modified 22 Oct 2024 (older render). Not a rolling window: always 1880 to latest year. Successors exist: 5450 (to 2024) and 5603 (to 2025).
- Status on 2026-11-14: static
- View / camera: Flat 2D chart on black, fixed axes, no map or globe, no camera motion.
- On-screen clutter: Year label, month axis, y-axis degC labels, green gridlines. The year number sits inside the plot area. No colorbar; colour encodes year (blue = old/cool, red = recent/warm), thick bright line = current year. No clean (label-free) variant on the page; F and Spanish variants only change labels.
- Fit as a sonification source: **good** — Black background with smooth coloured curves is easy to read by pixels and the time axis is simple (12 months x 145 years). But it is a chart, not a map, so the public GISTEMP table is a better source; the movie is a visual aid.
- Suggested mapping: Month on x = note in a 12-step scale; line height (anomaly + seasonal offset) = pitch; year = line colour / timbre brightness; play the final-year curve left to right as a melody and compare 1880 vs 2024 as two voices.

## Gotchas

- Y axis is the seasonal cycle (about -2.5 degC in Jan to +2 degC in Jul) plus anomaly, NOT pure anomaly: it mixes MERRA-2 offsets with GISTEMP anomalies.
- Not one frame per day; labels have year only.
- Frame set is EXR (not browser-decodable), numbered 2..1801 (index starts at 2) with copyEnd.csh/.py files in folder; folder contains stale Jan 2024 files.
- Superseded by 5450 / 5603; the 1880-2024 title means nothing after 2024.
- probe says ~1799 frames for degC but ~1801 for degF and Spanish.

Description: The visualization presents the seasonal cycle of temperature variation on the earth's surface. This visualization is updated roughly two weeks after the end of each month.
Temperature anomalies are deviations from a long term global average. In this case the period 1951-1980 is used to define the baseline for the anomaly. These temperatures are based on the GISS Surface Temperature Analysis (GISTEMP v4), an estimate of global surface temperature change. The data file used to create this visualization is publicaly accessible here.  The seasonal temperature offsets are based on the MERRA-2 reanalysis data here.
The Goddard Institute of Space Studies (GISS) is a NASA laboratory managed by the Earth Sciences Division of the agency’s Goddard Space Flight Center in Greenbelt, Maryland.
