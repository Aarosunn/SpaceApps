## 13786 — Data Sonification: Caldwell 73

Full sheet: [pages/13786.md](pages/13786.md) · https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/13786/

- **What it is:** NASA's own sonification example (official Jukebox resource; not Earth data and not in the EIC gallery tree): a 34 s square 1920x1920 produced video with a stereo music track and no on-screen text (no narration either, judging by the caption file's single music cue), in which a radar-style line sweeps once counterclockwise (12 deg/s, 30 s) around a static Hubble image of the globular cluster Caldwell 73 / NGC 1851 while stars trigger notes. SVS page type: Produced Video. Sonification credit: SYSTEM Sounds (M. Russo, A. Santaguida).
- **Kind:** other
- **Period covered:** not-applicable. A single static deep-space image, not a time series; the page gives no observation date (unknown). The only time axis is playback: 0 to 30 s = one sweep of the scan line, 30 to 34 s = still image while the sound dies away.
- **Data time step:** not-applicable (one still image; there is no data cadence).
- **One frame equals:** No real-world time at all. While the line sweeps (t < 30 s, frames 0 to about 899) one video frame = 1/30 s of playback = 0.40 deg of rotation of the scan line. Arithmetic (VERIFIED by measuring the line in the frames): angle -0.35 deg at t=0.01 s and 358.08 deg at t=29.87 s, so (358.08 + 0.35) / (29.87 - 0.01) = 358.43 / 29.86 = 12.00 deg/s; 12.00 / 30 fps = 0.40 deg per frame; 360 / 12.00 = 30.0 s = 900 frames per revolution. Frames from about 900 to 1019 (t = 30 to 34 s) are an unchanging hold with no line. Total length 34.00 s x 30 fps = 1020 frames (computed from the probe, not counted).
- **One frame = one day?:** not-applicable
- **Frame → time:** No date exists for any frame. The only thing a program can compute is frame index -> scan angle: t = k / 30 s; angle_deg = 12.0 * t = 0.40 * k, counted counterclockwise on screen from 3 o'clock (line pointing right at k = 0), pivot at the centre of the square frame; valid for k = 0 to about 899. From about k = 900 on there is no line. HOW THE SCAN MOVES (measured angles in deg): t=0.01 -> -0.35; 1.0 -> 11.40; 2.0 -> 23.18; 3.0 -> 35.07; 8.54 -> 102.46; 12.81 -> 153.92; 21.34 -> 255.35; 29.87 -> 358.08. Least-squares line through these 8 points: angle = 12.009 deg/s * t - 0.56 deg, largest residual 0.64 deg, so the speed is constant (no easing at the start or end) and there is exactly one revolution. The line is present at t=29.87 s and gone at t=30.5 s; the exact frame where it vanishes was not sampled.
- **Where the date comes from:** Nowhere (not-applicable). No date in any file name (Caldwell73_2K_Final.mp4, Caldwell73_2K_Final.webm, print.jpg, wide.jpg, SW.jpg, CALDWELL73.en_US.SRT), no date label in the picture, no date-only frame set, and no frame set of any kind. Only the index -> angle formula applies.
- **Date label on screen:** None. Nothing textual is burned into the picture in any of the 11 frames viewed (t = 0.01, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 8.54, 12.81, 21.34, 29.87, 30.5, 32.0, 33.8 s) or in NASA's still print.jpg: no date, year, time, time zone, title, units, colorbar, legend, credit line or NASA insignia.
- **Update behaviour:** Static, rendered once, never re-rendered. VERIFIED: all six files carry HTTP Last-Modified 2020-12-17 between 20:45:03 and 20:47:16 GMT (bundles/13786.md lines 35-40), and my own HEAD requests on 2026-11-14 (18:57 GMT and about 19:10 GMT) returned the same values (webm 20:47:16, mp4 20:45:17, print.jpg 20:45:04, SRT 20:45:09). The MP4 container's creation_time is 2020-12-16T00:20:36Z. Page released 2020-12-23; the page record was last touched 2023-05-03 (bundle line 5) without any change to the media files. No rolling window, no schedule.
- **View / camera:** Not a map and not a globe: a flat, static, square astronomical image filling a 1920x1920 frame, fixed camera, no zoom, pan or rotation of the picture (VERIFIED: the same stars sit on the same pixels in every sampled frame, for example the bright blue-white star near x=497, y=832 and the bright star near x=1247, y=524 in the 1280-px previews). The dense cluster core is at the frame centre, which is also the pivot of the scan line (best-fit pivot x=640, y=639 in the 1280x1280 previews). Thin black bars at the top and bottom, about 13 of 1280 preview rows each (roughly 1 percent of the height, about 20 px at full size); none at the left or right.
- **Clutter:** Only the sonification overlays, and only during the sweep (t < 30 s): (1) one thin whitish line from near the centre out to the frame edge, tinted violet-blue along parts of its outer half, with small bright blobs where it crosses stars; (2) small rings that flash around individual stars as the line passes them, trailing just behind the line and fading (whitish rings and bluish rings seen; INFERRED to mark the stars that trigger notes). From t = 30.5 s on the picture is clean. Never any text, colorbar, legend, inset or logo.
- **Clean variant:** No separate clean file, no alpha layer, no frame set. Workaround (VERIFIED in frames at t = 30.5, 32.0 and 33.8 s): from t = 30.5 s (possibly slightly earlier) to the end the movie shows the image with no line and no rings, so any frame there is a clean 1920x1920 still; take it from the MP4 for quality. NASA's stills are not clean: print.jpg and wide.jpg (same byte count, 1920x1080) are 16:9 crops of the central area with the scan line and rings burned in; SW.jpg is a 320x180 thumbnail.
- **Colours:** No colormap and no colorbar. Colours are the stars' own colours in the Hubble composite on a black sky: blue, white, yellow-orange and red points, with a dense white core; brightness is star brightness. Which filters map to which colour is not stated on the page (unknown). The page uses colour class to choose the instrument: orange and red stars -> marimba, blue stars -> glockenspiel.
- **Fit:** poor — Poor as a SOURCE, valuable as a REFERENCE. It is a deep-space Hubble still, not an Earth or EIC visualization (bundle line 6: not linked from the EIC gallery tree), it has no time axis, and a finished third-party sonification is already mixed into it, so it cannot be one of the jukebox's Earth tracks under the objective of sonifying EIC visual frames. What it is good for: it is the official worked example of a picture-to-sound mapping, and the picture itself is technically easy to sonify (sparse bright points on black, static, clean frames at t >= 30.5 s), which makes it a handy test image for a scan engine or for a short demo that reproduces NASA's own example. SOUND-ONLY FILE: none is downloadable; the page offers exactly six files (two movies, three JPEG stills, one caption file) and the music exists only as the audio track inside the two movies (Opus in the WebM, AAC-LC in the MP4).
- **Suggested mapping:** NASA'S MAPPING HERE (page text, bundles/13786.md line 29): a radar-style line scans around the image; star radius (distance from the centre) -> pitch, with stars farther from the centre sounding higher; the entire image is turned into a choir sound; orange and red stars are played on a marimba; blue stars on a glockenspiel. The page says nothing about loudness, stereo, scale or tempo. MEASURED IN THE AUDIO BY ME (not on the page): the sweep and the music both start on the first frame and last 30 s; every strong spectral peak from 82 Hz to 1.7 kHz sits within 2 cents of the notes E, F#, G#, B, C# (E major pentatonic) over a drone on E2 (82.4 Hz) and B2 (123.5 Hz), so pitch is snapped to a scale (INFERRED from the spectrum); the whole mix pans with the scan angle, hard right at about 4.4 s (line near 52 deg) and hard left at about 19.3 s (line near 231 deg); a rough detector counts about 270 note attacks in 30 s (about 9 per second). WHAT TO BORROW: (1) a visible cursor that shows where the playhead is, moving at constant speed; (2) a spatial coordinate -> pitch; (3) two layers: a continuous bed for the whole picture plus short notes for individual features; (4) colour class -> instrument; (5) pitches snapped to a pentatonic scale over a drone, so dense data still sounds pleasant; (6) a ring flash on the feature that just sounded, which ties ear to eye; (7) a piece length near 30 s. ONE MAPPING FOR AN EIC EARTH FRAME: sweep a vertical line west to east across a flat map in about 30 s (or keep the radar sweep for polar views); latitude along the line -> pitch on a pentatonic scale (north = high); pixel brightness or colorbar-decoded value -> loudness of a sustained pad; data layer or colour class -> instrument; isolated bright features -> mallet notes with a ring flash; stereo pan = screen x. SIBLING RECIPES by the same studio (svs_api/13786.json related list, lines 192-249): Bubble Nebula 13999 = top-to-bottom scan, colour -> pitch, brightness -> volume, stars as chimes; Butterfly Nebula 13998 = vertical position -> pitch, brightness -> volume, stars on digital harp; NGC 2392 13997 = clockwise radar scan, radius -> pitch, brightness -> volume; Hubble Ultra Deep Field 13893 = one note per galaxy, ordered by light-travel time.
- **Files to use:**

Safari support for WebM depends on the version and was not tested, so use the MP4 there. Hot-linked playback in a video tag works, but pixel or audio readback from another origin is blocked (see the CORS gotcha). — https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013700/a013786/Caldwell73_2K_Final.webm — Only 1386 kb/s overall for 1920x1920, so the star field is visibly smeared: fine for watching and listening, weak for pixel analysis. All preview frames and my audio measurements come from this file.
    - Master movie with soundtrack (MP4) — movie, 1920x1920 (square), 30 fps, 77.5 MB (77,451,214 bytes), 34.00 s, 1020 frames (34.00 s x 30 fps); video 17.7 Mb/s; browser: Yes natively in all current major browsers (H.264 + AAC in MP4). — https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013700/a013786/Caldwell73_2K_Final.mp4 — Use this one for pixel work, for a clean still (any frame at t >= 30.5 s) and for an audio-only copy (the AAC track can be copied out without re-encoding). Only its header was probed; no frames were extracted from it.

- **Gotchas:**
    - Not Earth data and not part of the EIC gallery: it is listed only as an official Jukebox resource (bundles/13786.md lines 6-7). Use it as a model and a test image, not as a jukebox track.
    - Challenge text vs page text. The challenge's resource blurb (SpaceApps/spaceapps-2026/sources/jukebox.txt line 28) and the team note (02-challenges-2026.md line 70) describe this page as luminosity and spatial coordinates turned into volume and pitch. The NASA page itself states only radius -> pitch, whole image -> choir, and star colour -> instrument; it never mentions brightness or volume. Brightness -> volume is stated on the sibling pages 13997, 13998 and 13999 (svs_api/13786.json lines 192, 211, 230), not here, and I could not confirm it from the audio. Do not cite this page as proof of a luminosity -> volume mapping.
    - Page vs files. The media description labels the video a horizontal master version for horizontal display (bundles/13786.md line 34), but both movie files are square, 1920x1920 (ffmpeg probe); only the stills are 16:9. The files win: lay it out as a square.
    - No audio-only file. To get the music alone, pull the audio track out of a movie (for example: ffmpeg -i Caldwell73_2K_Final.mp4 -vn -c:a copy caldwell73.m4a). The bundle's audio=True flag does not name the codecs: WebM = Opus 48 kHz stereo, MP4 = AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo 123 kb/s.
    - LOCAL BROWSER / CORS. svs.gsfc.nasa.gov answers every request with access-control-allow-origin: https://tempo.multiverse.music, a fixed value that ignores the caller's origin (VERIFIED with Origin http://localhost:8080, Origin null and no Origin header, on the webm, mp4, print.jpg, the SRT and /api/13786). CONSEQUENCE (standard browser CORS rules, not tested in a browser here): a page on localhost can still PLAY the NASA-hosted video in a plain video tag, but it cannot read its pixels through a canvas (tainted canvas), cannot analyse its audio with Web Audio, and cannot fetch() the file or the API JSON. Download the media once and serve it from the app's own origin. Run the app from a local web server (http://localhost), not as a file:// page, because browsers also block canvas readback of file:// media.
    - The movie is 34 s but the sonification is 30 s. The line is gone by t=30.5 s; the sound then decays (mid level -23.5 dBFS at 30.0-30.5 s, -41.5 at 31.0-31.5 s, below -60 dBFS after 32.1 s) and the last 2 s are silent. Loop or trim at 30 s, not at the file end.
    - Scan direction is not on the page. The text only says the radar scans around; the frames show counterclockwise from 3 o'clock. The sibling NGC 2392 page describes a clockwise scan, so direction is a per-piece choice, not a NASA convention.
    - Overlays are burned in for the whole sweep and there is no clean version of the sweeping part; NASA's still print.jpg also has the line in it and is a 16:9 crop.
    - The WebM is heavily compressed (1386 kb/s total for 1920x1920): stars are smeared and blocky. Fine for watching and for audio work, poor for per-pixel work; use the MP4 (17.7 Mb/s video) for that.
    - Stereo oddity. The left-right balance follows the scan angle in every frequency band, but it is hard right when the line points to about 52 deg (up and to the right, t about 4.4 s) and hard left at about 231 deg (t about 19.3 s), not when the line points straight right or left; it also starts near centre (+0.08) and ends at +0.35. Cause unknown. For our build, pan by screen x instead of copying this.
    - Accessibility of the official example is thin: the caption file holds a single cue (0 to 33.6 s) that is only a bracketed two-word tag calling the music pretty, there is no narration (INFERRED from that single cue and from the spectrum; nobody listened), and nothing on screen explains the mapping. A spoken or written legend, real captions and a downloadable audio file would already go beyond it.
    - Third-party credit. The sonification is credited to SYSTEM Sounds (M. Russo, A. Santaguida), not to NASA staff. If the clip or its audio appears in the demo, list it in References (team doc 03-rules-and-deadline.md lines 30, 34 and 67: credit all external resources). Reuse terms for the audio are not stated on the page.
    - Tooling: scripts/frames.sh cannot fetch a still JPEG (the bundled ffmpeg writes nothing when -ss 0 is applied to a single image and reports that the output file is empty), which is why frames/13786 holds no print.jpg; I viewed print.jpg from a temporary copy. frames.sh also prints only the video stream, so the audio details needed a direct ffmpeg probe.
    - Audio-derived statements (scale, pan, note density) come from my own analysis of the Opus track decoded from the WebM, not from NASA. The note-attack count is a rough estimate from a simple threshold detector, and the weaker peaks above 1.7 kHz are about 7 cents sharp and may be overtones.
    - Added by the second agent: The 1020 frame count is duration x fps (34.00 x 30), not a counted value; the webm at 34.01 s gives 1020.3. Index frames by time, not by a hard-coded count.

## 5313 — Change in Night Lights between 2012 and 2023 - EIC Version

Full sheet: [pages/5313.md](pages/5313.md) · https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5313/

- **What it is:** A 43.33 s, 7680x2160 (32:9) loop: 12 annual-average Black Marble night-light maps (2012 to 2023) flash by in the first 6.4 s, the lights fade into a purple/orange map of the 2012-to-2023 change, then the same sequence repeats with four zoomed pop-out boxes (Western Europe, Eastern Mediterranean, Ukraine, India). Every frame shows the whole world twice side by side. It is 13 distinct pictures, not one image per day.
- **Kind:** data-animation
- **Period covered:** Calendar years 2012 to 2023 as 12 annual averages (VERIFIED: every label 2012, 2013, ... 2023 was read off the decoded frames), plus one derived map of the change between 2012 and 2023. No month, day or time of day exists anywhere in this product.
- **Data time step:** 1 year (annual average); 12 steps. The change map is one still picture (2023 compared with 2012) with no time step.
- **One frame equals:** Nothing fixed - this is not a regular time-lapse. The movie has 1300 frames at 30 fps (1300/30 = 43.33 s) but only 13 distinct data pictures (12 annual maps + 1 change map), each shown twice. While the years step, one data step (1 year) is held for 17 or 18 movie frames: the label changes at frames 15, 33, 51, 69, 86, 104, 122, 139, 157, 175, 193, so the mean hold is (193-15)/10 = 17.8 frames = 0.593 s per year; nominally 1 frame = 1/17.8 year = 20.5 days, but the picture does not move inside a hold (lights and label switch together within 1-2 frames, no cross-fade between years). Exceptions: 2012 is held 15 frames (0.50 s) in pass 1 and 187 frames (6.23 s) in pass 2; 2023 is held 83 frames (2.77 s) at full strength in each pass. Of the 1300 frames, 724 (55.7%) show a year at full strength, 491 (37.8%) show the static change map and 85 (6.5%) are fades.
- **One frame = one day?:** no
- **Frame → time:** VERIFIED by decoding all 1300 frames of the HEVC movie once and measuring the centre label box, the colour-bar box and the four inset boxes in every frame. k = 0-based movie frame, t = k/30 s. PASS 1 (no boxes): 2012 k=0-14 (0.000-0.500 s); 2013 k=15-32 (from 0.500 s); 2014 k=33-50 (1.100 s); 2015 k=51-68 (1.700 s); 2016 k=69-85 (2.300 s); 2017 k=86-103 (2.867 s); 2018 k=104-121 (3.467 s); 2019 k=122-138 (4.067 s); 2020 k=139-156 (4.633 s); 2021 k=157-174 (5.233 s); 2022 k=175-192 (5.833 s); 2023 k=193-275 at full strength (6.433-9.200 s), fading out k=276-289; change map + colour bar fading in k=290-304 (9.667-10.167 s), full k=305-615 (10.167-20.533 s; 311 frames = 10.37 s), fading out k=616-629. PASS 2: 2012 fading in k=630-642 (21.000-21.433 s), 2012 full k=643-829; the four zoom boxes pop out together during k=670-686 (22.333-22.867 s) and stay to the last frame; 2013 k=830-847 (27.667 s); 2014 k=848-865 (28.267 s); 2015 k=866-883 (28.867 s); 2016 k=884-900 (29.467 s); 2017 k=901-918 (30.033 s); 2018 k=919-936 (30.633 s); 2019 k=937-953 (31.233 s); 2020 k=954-971 (31.800 s); 2021 k=972-989 (32.400 s); 2022 k=990-1007 (33.000 s); 2023 k=1008-1090 full (33.600-36.367 s), fading out k=1091-1104; change map fading in k=1105-1119 (36.833-37.333 s), full k=1120-1299 (37.333-43.333 s; 180 frames = 6.0 s). Every pass-2 year change is exactly the pass-1 change + 815 frames. FORMULA: if k<=289 let j=k; if 630<=k<=1104 let j=k-815; then year = 2012 + min(11, max(0, floor((j-14.875)/17.725)+1)); any other k has no year (change map). The formula reproduces all 11 measured change frames. EXR frame-set file number N = 2k+1 (odd numbers 00001 to 02599 only), so k = (N-1)/2: VERIFIED at the box pop-out (EXR 01339 = 21.34 MB, 01341 = 29.69 MB; movie frames 669 and 670).
- **Where the date comes from:** VERIFIED options: (1) compute it from the frame index with the table or formula in frame_to_time_mapping (recommended; exact); (2) OCR the burned-in 4-digit year (present only on k=0-289 and k=630-1104). File names never contain the year: the movie names have none and the EXR names carry only a render frame number (for example ...EIC.02599.exr). There is no date-only or 'dates' frame set on this page (one frame folder only). On the alternate page 5276 the year IS in the file name (nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k_only_lights.2012.tif ... .2023.tif and nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k.02012.tif ... .02023.tif).
- **Date label on screen:** Yes, year only: a light-grey 4-digit label (2012 ... 2023) just right of the centre seam, at about x 3980-4155, y 1070-1145 of the 7680x2160 frame (x 663-692, y 178-191 in the 1280x360 previews). No month, day, clock time or time zone. It is absent during the change map (k=290-629 and 1105-1299), where the only date text is the colour-bar title 'Change in Lighting Intensity from 2012 to 2023'.
- **Update behaviour:** Static: rendered once and never refreshed. Evidence: EXR header capDate 2024:05:30 15:21:38 (render time); upload Last-Modified values all on 2024-06-14 UTC (EXR frames 19:08:45 to 19:17:54, h265 mp4 19:09:42, ProRes 19:10:35, searchweb and thumbnail 19:51, print.jpg 19:53:06); page released 2024-06-14 and page record last updated 2024-06-14T15:53:06-04:00 (= 19:53:06 UTC). No file is newer, so nothing has changed for 883 days up to 2026-11-14 (365 + 365 + 153). Not a rolling window; the newest data is the 2023 annual average; there is no lag to report.
- **View / camera:** Flat equirectangular, fixed geography and camera; the 7680x2160 frame holds the whole world twice as two 3840x2160 tiles (10.67 px per deg lon, 12 px per deg lat). Left edge of each tile is about -168.6 deg (+/-0.4); the analyst's -168.4 +/-0.3 and the team's -168.8 both fall inside that error (about 4 px apart). lon = -168.6 + 360*(x mod 3840)/3840, lat = 90 - 180*y/2160. The offset is a fit, not exact.
- **Clutter:** No NASA logo, title, credits or numeric scale anywhere. (1) Year label just right of the centre seam, so it lies entirely in the right tile (k=0-289 and 630-1104). (2) Colour bar across the centre seam at about x 3764-4370, y 1180-1285 (k=290-629 and 1105-1299): title 'Change in Lighting Intensity from 2012 to 2023', captions 'lights out', 'no change', 'new lights'; no numbers. (3) From k=670 to the last frame, four pop-out boxes with thin white outlines, a small source rectangle on the map and a translucent connector wedge, titled 'Western Europe' and 'Eastern Mediterranean' (left tile) and 'Ukraine' and 'India' (right tile). Together they alter 22.5% of the frame. Bounding boxes including wedge and source rectangle, in 1280x360 preview pixels (multiply by 6 for full resolution): Western Europe x 112-334, y 64-343; Eastern Mediterranean x 346-539, y 19-213; Ukraine x 770-1013, y 16-155; India x 1008-1181, y 112-350. The boxes are magnified copies, so their lights are bigger and brighter than on the map (whole-frame mean luma jumps 42.8% when they appear), and they hide Central/South America and most of Asia on the left tile and eastern North America and the Indian Ocean on the right tile.
- **Clean variant:** Yes by cropping; no as a separate official file on this page. VERIFIED: the left tile (crop 3840x2160 at x=0) of any frame k<=289 is a complete world map with no text at all (checked on frame 7), because the year label lies in the right tile; in the first change-map hold (k=305-615) the left tile only catches the left 76 px of the colour bar at its right edge (x 3764-3839, y 1180-1285). Page 5313 offers no label-free movie, no date-free frame set and no separate layers (the EXR frames do have an alpha channel; its content was not inspected). Purpose-made clean files are on alternate page 5276 (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5276/, checked live today, not in the evidence bundle), all under https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a005200/a005276/ : 12 lights-only TIFFs on black, one per year with the year in the name (16-bit RGBA with alpha); the same 12 frames as a 1.07 MB movie nightlights_flat_series_stills_only_lights_2160p3_h265.mp4 (frame i = year 2012+i, but a small year label is burned in); 12 with-basemap TIFFs nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k.02012.tif ... .02023.tif (62 MB each as listed); 8K JPEG stills, HEAD-checked but not opened: change map without colour bar nightlights_flat_change_still_8k.00700.jpg (2648109 bytes), change map on black nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k_only_lights.change.jpg (1956065 bytes), 2012 and 2023 comparison images nightlights_flat_series_stills_8k_compare.02012.jpg (2466015 bytes) and .02023.jpg (2606413 bytes); the colour bar alone nightlights_change_colorbar.png (3840x2160, 684708 bytes).
- **Colours:** Time-series part: warm yellow-white points = night-light brightness from the annual average (brighter and whiter = more light); no legend, no units; values are display-scaled, not radiances. They sit on a very dark grey land / black ocean base map (ice sheets lighter grey). Change part: diverging colours on the same base map - orange = less light in 2023 than in 2012 (bar caption 'lights out'), white = 'no change', purple = more light ('new lights'). The bar has no numbers or units.
- **Fit:** good — Good as a SPATIAL source, weak as a time series. Fixed geography, a nearly black background (whole-frame mean luma about 8 of 255) and sparse bright points make brightness easy to turn into sound, the change map has a clear two-sided meaning (purple = more light, orange = less), and the challenge text names this page. But there are only 13 distinct pictures, and year-to-year differences are small: measured on the movie, whole-frame mean luma rises only 2.98% from 2012 to 2023 (7.946 to 8.183), the Asia box (about 54-134 deg E) 9.6%, the share of bright pixels 43% (0.0757% to 0.1083% at 960x270). A naive 'frame brightness to volume' mapping on the movie would mostly play the fades and the zoom-box pop-out (+42.8% luma at frames 670-686), not the data. The page's own files are awkward (8K-wide HEVC, 4.29 GB ProRes, 1300 EXR files of 21-36 MB), so work from 13 extracted stills or from the 5276 per-year files.
- **Suggested mapping:** Treat it as 12 yearly images + 1 change image, not as video. Take one 3840x2160 tile (left half, frames without boxes). Sweep a playhead west to east across the 360 deg of longitude; for each longitude column sum the light in latitude bands: band = pitch on a pentatonic scale (north = high), summed brightness = loudness and note density, longitude = stereo pan. Play the 12 years as 12 bars so growth (India, China, the Gulf) becomes audibly denser. - **Kind:** data-animation
- **Period covered:** VERIFIED: model time 2007-03-25T12:00Z to 2008-03-03T12:00Z = exactly 344 days (about 11.3 months; 22 days short of a full year; includes 29 Feb 2008). - **One frame equals:** 4.128 hours of model time (4 h 07 min 40.8 s = 14,860.8 s = 0.172 day). Arithmetic: 344 days x 24 = 8256 h; 2001 frames = 2000 intervals; 8256 / 2000 = 4.128 h. - **Frame → time:** Let k = 0-based frame index, 0..2000. time(k) = 2007-03-25T12:00:00Z + k x 14860.8 s (use real UTC date arithmetic; 2008 is a leap year).
