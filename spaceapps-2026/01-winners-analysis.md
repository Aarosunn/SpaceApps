## How judging works (funnel + criteria + award definitions)

Primary source: Space Apps 2022 Judging & Awards Guide PDF (official, hosted on Space Apps S3): https://spaceapps-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/Space_Apps_2022_Judging_and_Awards_Guide.pdf [VERIFIED, read pages 1-8]. The 2025/2026 guide (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide) was unreadable (TLS error). Search snippets for the 2025 guide confirm the same five criteria (Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, Presentation) and say teams should "give equal attention to presentation" [VERIFIED via search snippet only, not the page itself].

Funnel (2022 guide) [VERIFIED]:
1. Local judging: each local event picks **1-3** projects -> "Global Nominees". Universal Event: ~40 SMEs pick nominees, "usually around one in ten" of the number advancing from local events.
2. Global judging: NASA + partner-agency experts score nominees -> "Global Finalists".
3. Executive judging: agency leaders score every finalist and assign the 10 awards.
=> You must first WIN YOUR LOCAL EVENT (top 1-3). Local judges are the first gate [INFERRED].

Eligibility gotchas (2022) [VERIFIED]: must answer an official challenge (Create-Your-Own not eligible for global; same in 2025 per search snippet); max 6 members; English on the project page and all linked material; non-English video OK only WITH English subtitles; all external code/text/images/music credited in References; no copyrighted music/images without permission (relevant for a sonification project: use original or licensed audio) .

Criteria (verbatim, 2022) [VERIFIED]:
- Impact: "How much impact (quality and quantity) can this project have? Does it solve a big problem or a little problem? Will it inspire or help many, or a few?"
- Creativity: "Is the project novel and something that hasn't been attempted before, or is it an incremental improvement on something that already exists?"
- Validity: "Is the solution scientifically valid? Will it do what it sets out to do? Can it work in the real world?"
- Relevance: "Is this project responsive to the challenge ... Is it a complete solution or does it have a long way to go? Is it technically feasible? How usable or user friendly is the solution?"
- Presentation: "How well did the team communicate their project? ... telling the story of the project: the challenge, the solution, and why is it important?"

Award definitions (verbatim, 2022) [VERIFIED]:
- Best Use of Science: "best and most valid use of science and/or the scientific method."
- Best Use of Data: "best makes space data accessible, or leverages it to a unique application."
- Best Use of Technology: "most innovative use of technology."
- Galactic Impact: "most potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe."
- Best Mission Concept: "most plausible concept and design."
- Most Inspirational: "captures our hearts."
- Best Storytelling: "most creatively communicates the potential of open data through the art of storytelling."
- Global Connection: "best connects people around the world through technology."
- Art & Technology: "most effectively combines technical and creative skills."
- Local Impact: "greatest potential for local impact."

Takeaways [INFERRED]:
- Sonification HAS won twice (2018 Most Inspirational, 2023 Global Connection). Both winners framed the audio as **accessibility for blind / low-vision people**, not just "space sounds cool". Neither won Science/Data/Technology.
- The common naive approach (scan image left->right, brightness -> volume, y -> pitch) is what most entries do (SPACETUNE, SuperSonic per Grace Xu's portfolio https://gracewxu.netlify.app/work/sonification). It got SPACETUNE to Global Nominee, not further. To stand out: a mapping that carries *information* (listener can answer a question from the sound: "is this year hotter than average?", "where is the drought?"), ideally with a tiny listening test showing people can decode it -> that hits Validity.
- NASA itself does sonification (Chandra "A Universe of Sound"; published listener study https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2024.1288896 reports positive responses from sighted and blind/low-vision groups [search snippet, not read in full]). Citing NASA's own sonification work + its accessibility evidence is cheap credibility.
- Award fit for a Jukebox project: Global Connection (accessibility, connecting people), Art & Technology ("combines technical and creative skills"), Most Inspirational, Storytelling. Best Use of Data is possible if the mapping clearly makes Earth data *accessible* (literally the award definition: "best makes space data accessible").
- Credit/licensing: the guide requires no copyrighted music without permission; generate your own audio [VERIFIED rule, 2022 guide].

## Winner deep-dives and post-mortems

### 2024 Best Use of Science: WMPGang / "SkyShield" (Waterloo event, Western U)
Source: https://news.westernu.ca/2025/01/western-students-win-nasa-challenge-with-app-shows-risks-to-space-infrastructure/ [VERIFIED]; https://magazine.westernu.ca/out-of-this-world-win/ [VERIFIED]
- 4 physics/astronomy grad students; **none had web-dev experience**; one learned JavaScript 2 days before. Built in ~30 h. Plain HTML + JS; ChatGPT used for debugging/HTML templates.
- 3D orrery plotting 1,669 NEOs + meteoroid streams + sporadic meteoroids + 8 planets + 5 dwarf planets; filters (higher-risk only, eccentric orbits).
- Differentiator (team quote): most orreries show planets and at most one asteroid; they showed many + added the overlooked **meteoroid risk to space infrastructure** angle. Leader Vovk chose meteoroids deliberately because he thought it "would be crucial to winning".
- Validity: sporadic-meteoroid component based on the meteoroid environment model **NASA uses in mission planning**. A physics professor praised the dataset choices.
- Western magazine: competed against 15,444 teams.
- Lesson [INFERRED]: domain expertise + a recognized NASA model beat web polish for the Science award. They took the obvious challenge build (orrery) and added ONE scientifically meaningful twist.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. Winners are tightly scoped to one challenge with obvious, visible NASA data use. -> SUPPORTED (with nuance).**
- Eligibility literally requires answering one official challenge; Create-Your-Own is ineligible for global (2022 guide) [VERIFIED]. Relevance criterion asks "Is this project responsive to the challenge?" [VERIFIED].
- Every winner in 2023-2025 maps to exactly one named challenge (NASA pages) [VERIFIED]. Data use is named in summaries: MODIS, JWST spectra, NEO + meteor streams, soil moisture + evapotranspiration, Apollo seismometers, DSCOVR [VERIFIED].
- Nuance: several winners are concepts/proposals with lighter data (Photonics Odyssey satellite-internet sovereignty proposal; ASTROGENESIS tourism itineraries; Oogway Comics) [VERIFIED summaries]. Visible data matters most for Science/Data/Technology awards [INFERRED].

**H2. The idea is understandable in 30 seconds. -> SUPPORTED.**
- Every NASA one-line summary of a winner is a single sentence with a clear user + payoff ("help farmers manage planting risks", "flags satellite risk zones", "teach children how space weather affects daily life") [VERIFIED]. Presentation criterion: "telling the story ... the challenge, the solution, and why is it important" [VERIFIED].
- Executive judges are "leaders and executives ... with a variety of subject matter expertise" and score every finalist [VERIFIED 2022 guide] => non-specialists in your topic will judge you [INFERRED].

**H3. Each award rewards a different thing; pick a target award on purpose. -> SUPPORTED.**
Award -> what winners had in common (2023-2025) [INFERRED from VERIFIED summaries]:
- Science: domain-correct modeling with a recognized reference (NASA meteoroid model, Apollo seismic data, molecular/omics). Winners often have domain experts (physics grads; 1-person bio). 
- Data: makes data *accessible* or uses it for prediction: LSTM geomagnetic storm prediction (DSCOVR), farmer water management, JWST-spectra AI pipeline.
- Technology: ML + signal processing or AI forecasting with a real engineering purpose (seismic DNN for landers, hyperlocal weather AI, moonquake integration site).
- Galactic Impact: big-problem framing with a usable tool (debris compliance index, farmer planting risk, ocean data viz).
- Mission Concept: a plausible designed system/platform (habitat layout + space-weather testing; Landsat Connect; tourism itinerary platform).
- Most Inspirational: education/kids/emotion or national-development story (eclipse tool for children, SDG classroom city-builder game, sovereign satellite internet for villages, SongSAT 2018 accessibility music).
- Storytelling: narrative artifacts: children's illustrated book, climate stories across regions + game, water-cycle story game.
- Global Connection: accessibility / global non-expert audience: sonification for blind users (2023), exoplanet explorer for "a global audience" (2024), data-center network co-design (2025).
- Art & Technology: game, comic, virtual museum — an artistic medium carrying real data.
- Local Impact: a specific named place and community (Arequipa queñua forests; Florianópolis community GIS; Taiwan biodiversity hotspots).
- Also: the same challenge produced multiple winners in different awards each year (LEO x3 in 2025; Agriculture EO x2 in 2024; Moonquake x2 in 2023) [VERIFIED counts] => judges spread awards across angles, so a clearly-positioned angle matters more than picking an "uncrowded" challenge [INFERRED].

**H4. Scientific validity (benchmarks, known values) separates winners from pretty dashboards. -> PARTLY SUPPORTED / UNCLEAR.**
- Validity is one of 5 criteria; Science award is defined as "most valid use of science" [VERIFIED].
- SkyShield used NASA's mission-planning meteoroid model; Celara (nominee) claimed fewer false positives vs single-input models [VERIFIED]. 
- But public winner summaries rarely cite accuracy numbers, and several winners are explicitly dashboards, games, or stories [VERIFIED]. I could not read project pages to check whether they reported benchmarks [UNKNOWN]. Best reading: validity is a gate for Science/Data/Technology; for the other 7 awards, clarity and audience fit dominate [INFERRED].

**H5. Crowded, obvious builds (generic dashboards, chatbots over NASA data) rarely win. -> SUPPORTED (indirect evidence).**
- Cincinnati 2025: 3 teams built space-bio paper search/RAG; 4 built exoplanet AI; none became global winners; the winners of those challenges went beyond the obvious build [VERIFIED/INFERRED].
- 2023 sonification challenge: 10+ public repos; 1 winner, with an accessibility angle [VERIFIED/INFERRED].
- 2025 "Will It Rain": multiple look-alike entries (even same name SkySense) [search snippet]; winner added activity risk ratings + AI [VERIFIED].
- Counterpoint: winners DO build in crowded challenges; WMPGang built the obvious orrery but added a meteoroid-risk twist [VERIFIED]. So "obvious challenge + one non-obvious, meaningful twist" works; "obvious build, no twist" doesn't [INFERRED].

## Top 10 actionable lessons
1. **Win the room first.** Only 1-3 projects per local event advance; optimize the local judging pitch and demo (2022 guide).
2. **Pick your target award on purpose** and write the award's definition on the whiteboard. For a Jukebox/sonification project the realistic targets are Global Connection, Art & Technology, Most Inspirational, or Best Use of Data ("makes space data accessible").
3. **Take the obvious build and add ONE meaningful twist** (SkyShield: many orbits + meteoroid risk; Celara: star properties to cut false positives; 2023 sonification winner: accessibility for blind users). Generic dashboard/chatbot/left-to-right image scan = invisible.
4. **One-sentence pitch with a named user and payoff.** Every winner summary fits in one sentence. Write it before coding.
5. **Show NASA data by name, on screen** (dataset + mission + date range) and in References. Relevance + Validity both reward it.
6. **Anchor to a recognized NASA reference for validity**: a NASA model, a known event, or known values the audience can check (e.g., "hear the 2023 heat record"/known El Niño years). For sonification, include a tiny listener test ("4 of 5 people identified the hotter decade by ear").
7. **Design for non-experts and a global audience** (executive judges are generalist leaders; Global Connection winners stressed accessibility). Add captions/visual sync + screen-reader-friendly UI.
8. **Presentation is weighted like the rest**: dedicate a presenter; a tight 30-second demo video with English subtitles; story = problem, solution, why it matters.
9. **Compliance is free points**: English page, all members registered, references for every external asset, **no copyrighted music** (generate your own sounds), official challenge only.
10. **Scope to a demo that works end-to-end in 30 h** (SkyShield: plain HTML/JS, learned on the spot). A working narrow thing beats a broad unfinished platform ("Is it a complete solution?" is in Relevance).
