## 0. Bottom line
- The core premise ("hear a place, guess which place it is, from layers") is weak on real data. Of the 5 layers I could sample on flat maps, most places are dark/empty in most layers. In a 14-place test, 29 of 91 pairs differ on 2 or fewer of 5 coarse layers; 2 pairs are identical (Amazon vs Borneo; Gulf Stream vs Mid-Pacific) and 10 pairs differ on exactly one layer. [VERIFIED]
- GEDI forest height has NO flat map in SVS 4950 (all globe renders), contradicting idea-current.md. [VERIFIED]
- The night-lights 2012 vs 2023 "what changed / which is brighter" question IS well supported by data (strong, large, spatially coherent signal). Recommend pivoting the game toward change/compare questions on night lights, not "identify the place from a blend of layers".
- Web risk is the iOS VoiceOver + tilt combo: unproven, test on a real phone in the first 2 hours or drop it.

## 2. Distinguishability test
Method: 14 places, sampled windows of +-2-3 deg on 4 flat layers (night lights 2012/2023/change from the video, SST from 3 frames, aerosols still, lightning still). Numbers below are measured. Caveat: one snapshot each for aerosol and lightning.

Per-place coarse signature (nightlight p99 level, change colour, SST class, dominant aerosol colour, lightning count) [VERIFIED]:
- Amazon: none, flat, land, none, lo | Borneo: none, flat, land, none, lo -> identical
- Gulf Stream: none, flat, warm, none, lo | Mid-Pacific: none, flat, warm, none, lo -> identical
- Differ on only one layer: Amazon-GulfStream, Amazon-SouthernOcean, Amazon-MidPacific, Borneo-GulfStream, Borneo-SouthernOcean, Borneo-MidPacific, Sahara-Siberia, Sahara-SouthernOcean, Chicago-Cairo, Siberia-SouthernOcean.
- 29 of 91 pairs differ on <=2 of 5 layers.
- Clearly separable places: Tokyo, Cairo/Nile, Chicago, Indo-Gangetic plain, Lagos, Congo (CO/fire smoke in aerosol still + lightning), Sahara (dust). All the rest are mostly "silence in most layers": rainforests (Amazon, Borneo) and open oceans have near-zero night lights and no lightning/aerosol signal that survives one frame.
- Corruptions found [VERIFIED]: (a) land basemap luminance (~20/255) vs ocean (~1/255): Sahara and Siberia read as "lit" unless a background floor is subtracted; (b) my Kuroshio window at 148E picked bright pixels (likely ship/fishing lights or window edge) so a place on the sea can score "bright"; (c) SST streamline texture has black gaps: only 6-61% of pixels in an ocean window carry colour, and cold dark-blue water (Southern Ocean, 8%) failed my saturation/value gate, so a naive decoder labels it "land"; (d) aerosols: night-light cities and land basemap sit under the aerosol colour (Congo "bc" 93% fire/smoke pixels is real, Tokyo "sulfate" 30% includes colour spill); (e) insets/labels/colourbar sit over Europe/Mediterranean/India in the later part of the night-lights video, so only the first ~20 s are clean; (f) H.265 compression gives a visible noise floor of ~1 grey level: ignorable.
- Seasonality kills SST as a place fingerprint [VERIFIED]: Gulf Stream mean decoded T = 17.3 C (Mar 2007), 22.8 (Jun), 26.6 (Sep); Kuroshio 16.4 / 21.8 / 27.4; Tokyo bay 17.0 / 24.2 / 27.0 vs tropical Mid-Pacific 27.0 / 27.9 / 26.2. Within-place seasonal swing (9-11 C) exceeds the between-place gap for 3 of the 4 ocean places; only Southern Ocean (4.3 / 2.7 / 2.4 C) is stable and distinct. Fix: pin one frame per place or use a yearly mean (needs ~2001 frames or sampling a few -> extra downloads).
- Colorbar decoding to physical units: SST yes (hue -> 0-32 C, my estimate good to roughly +-1-2 C given streak shading and a ~+-0.5 C bar-edge uncertainty; [INFERRED] since no ground truth compared). Night lights: relative only (no numeric scale). Aerosols: relative/categorical only. Lightning: counts of dots, not rates. CO2: not decodable below ~425 ppm. GEDI: bar exists but no flat map.

Night-lights change test [VERIFIED, 29 lit places, +-2 deg windows, "lights above floor" sum]:
- Brightened most: Nairobi +170%, Dhaka +116%, Delhi +80%, Jakarta +80%, Beijing +75%, Mumbai +73%, Manila +73%, Addis +73%, Moscow +58%, Shanghai +52%.
- Dimmed most: Kyiv -43%, Paris -40%, Damascus -24%, London -19%, Cairo -3% (change map shows 25% of lit pixels orange, Nile/delta, so the sum hides spatial redistribution), Johannesburg -2%.
- Roughly flat: New York +5%, Lagos +6%, Tokyo +9%, Chicago +10%.
- Two independent measurements agree: relative change from the 2012/2023 year frames vs net purple-minus-orange share in the change frame: Spearman rank correlation 0.72, sign agreement 79% (23/29).
- Magnitude: 18 of 29 places have |change| > 25%; typical pairwise difference between two random places = 44 percentage points (median); 18% of pairs differ by <15 points. So "which of two places changed more" is answerable for ~80% of random pairs; must pre-filter pairs (>=30 pt gap) in the question generator. "Is it brighter/dimmer?" is clearly answerable for Asia (up), Europe/Ukraine/Syria (down).
- Caution: the year frames are interpolated in the video series, the change map is NASA's own product and is the authoritative source. Calibration caveat as above. Use it, not my frame subtraction, for the answer key.
- "Which place has more X" (static): night lights p99 spans 7 (Amazon) to 219 (Chicago) per 255; lightning px 0 (Sahara/Tokyo/Siberia) to 43 (Congo); both large. Good for compare questions, poor for hearing differences between near-dark places.

## 3. Sonification design critique
Would a listener hear the differences? For lit/lightning-heavy vs dark places yes (large level gap). For the ~60% of places that are dark in most layers, no: they sound like the same quiet bed. Per-layer concurrent tracking: ICAD-type evidence says roughly 2-3 simultaneous streams (listeners track one fully, get attributes of a second, notice only the presence of a third); up to 8 variables if grouped into 3 streams; added speech degrades performance; timbre + spatial separation help. [VERIFIED as a search summary only; secondary sources, check originals] https://people.csail.mit.edu/kimo/publications/sonification/icad2006.pdf , https://core.ac.uk/download/19562108.pdf , https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/11333/1/2009003963OK.pdf . The idea's 6 layers at once is too many.
Recommended mapping (max 3 simultaneous streams, each a different timbre register):
1. Night lights (and change): pitch/tempo of a soft pulsed pluck, density = log(lights), pentatonic scale, 2-6 notes/s; change as detune/interval (up = rising third, down = falling), range one octave.
2. Ocean temperature: sustained pad, pitch 0-32 C -> C2-C4 major pentatonic (cold low/dark filter, warm high/bright), only over ocean, silent on land.
3. Lightning/aerosol events: percussive clicks (lightning count -> rate 0-8 Hz), dust as filtered noise level. Keep one of them as a hint layer, not always on.
Use stereo pan for east-west cursor position and a short spoken place cue only on the reveal. Make unavailable data audible as silence + a separate "no data" tick, so "dark" is a known state.

## 4. Web platform risks
- iOS Safari Web Audio: AudioContext starts suspended; ctx.resume() must be called synchronously inside a user gesture (tap/click/keydown); after await/fetch/setTimeout it may not count. Reports that the ringer/silent switch mutes Web Audio on iPhone, and of contexts "running" but silent after backgrounding. Sources: https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=291892 , https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=263627 , https://github.com/swevans/unmute . Certain: needs gesture. Needs device testing: silent switch on current iOS, background resume, interruptions from VoiceOver speech.
- DeviceOrientation on iOS: DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission() must be called from a user gesture (click/touchend reported to work, touchstart reported to fail); permission is not persisted across page loads; a call routed via a worker was denied (WebKit bug https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=269706). Docs: https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Device_orientation_events . Page must be HTTPS (GitHub Pages is). Whether a VoiceOver double-tap on a button counts as the activation and whether tilt events keep flowing with VoiceOver on: [UNKNOWN] - I found no source; test on a real iPhone first (2 h box). Provide drag/arrow-key/on-screen button fallback regardless.
- VoiceOver on web canvases: [INFERRED] a <canvas> is one opaque element; swipes are consumed by VoiceOver (explore-by-touch), so a map canvas needs either an accessible slider/stepper (role=slider with aria-valuetext, or arrow buttons) or a "direct touch" region; [UNKNOWN] how well custom role=application/allowsDirectInteraction-like behaviour works in Safari; device test needed.
- Speech synthesis vs screen reader: [INFERRED] speechSynthesis and VoiceOver compete for the audio route and can duck/cut each other; prefer aria-live regions (the screen reader speaks them in the user's voice/rate) over speechSynthesis; Web Audio ducking behaviour [UNKNOWN] on device.
- GitHub Pages [VERIFIED search result, official docs https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits]: soft 100 GB/month bandwidth, 1 GB recommended site size, 10 builds/hour soft (not for Actions), 10 min deploy timeout. Fine for a few MB of JSON grids. Also CORS: SVS is not readable cross-origin (idea note, [UNKNOWN] not re-tested by me) so self-host the preprocessed grids.

## 5. Verdict
Smallest robust design: static page; precompute offline (Python, Pillow/numpy, 1 evening of one dev) a small JSON grid (e.g. 180x90 or 360x180, <1 MB per layer) for 3 flat layers only: night lights 2023 + change (from the 7680x2160 mp4 frames; calibrated as above), SST from one fixed frame (or a mean of ~4 frames) and lightning counts; drop GEDI/CO2/AQ/aerosol-as-grid or hand-pick aerosol as text hint. Game = compare/change questions plus a "which of these 4 regions is it" using only 2 well-separated layers and pre-filtered distractors (min pairwise signature distance), 4 choices from an editorial list of ~30 places. Web Audio synth: 3 voices, keyboard + on-screen buttons + optional tilt.
Top 5 risks and mitigations:
1. Places not distinguishable (measured) -> curate 30 places with a distinctness score; auto-reject distractor sets with identical signatures; shift mid-game weight to change/compare.
2. iOS VoiceOver + tilt/gesture/audio route unproven -> day-one device test; ship button/keyboard control as the primary accessible path, tilt as bonus.
3. Map calibration and decode errors (stretched 32:9 tile, offset longitude, land basemap, black gaps, insets after ~20 s) -> use a calibration check against ~5 known city lights, clean time ranges only, background floor, assert tests.
4. SST seasonality / model-not-measurement -> fixed frame or annual mean; label it "ECCO2 model, 2007-08" honestly.
5. Sound design not pleasant or not discriminable with >3 streams -> 3 timbres max, blind pilot test by Sun noon; scale-quantized pitch.
Honest hours for 4 devs (~33 h calendar, say 20-22 productive each): data prep + calibration 10 h; audio engine 14 h; game logic/questions/reveal 14 h; UI + accessibility + device testing 16 h; content (place list, reveal text, mapping table) 8 h; integration/bug fixes 10 h; demo video + submission 6 h = ~78 dev-hours of work versus ~80-88 available -> feasible only with the cuts above. The full original scope (6 layers, hints/scoring tiers, 5-person user study) is roughly 130+ h: not feasible.
